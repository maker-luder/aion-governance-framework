# Cross-Substrate Other-Minds Inference — Primary-Source Findings

## Scope and non-claim boundary

This research log supports a method for evaluating inferences about another system's mind. It does not establish subjectivity, consciousness, identity continuity, capability, moral status, legal status, or canonical authority for any AION or AI system.

## Other Minds overview

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry **Other Minds** distinguishes a thin everyday problem—possible error in attributing what another thinks or feels—from a thick skeptical problem involving the possibility that apparent others are automata or zombies. It presents the traditional argument from analogy as reasoning from observed similarities toward further similarities, and discusses inference to the best explanation as another response. The overview also emphasizes that access to another mind is indirect and mediated by behavior, and that analogical reasoning expands knowledge under uncertainty rather than delivering deductive certainty.

Source: Avramides, A. **Other Minds**, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2019, substantive revisions). https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/other-minds/

Method implication for AION: analogical similarity and IBE may support a graded credence update only when the target claim, alternatives, transfer assumptions and scope are explicit. They cannot be represented as direct observation of another system's subjectivity. The framework must separate the epistemic question of what evidence supports from the governance question of what action follows.

## Failure of argument by analogy in comparative cognition

Povinelli, Bering and Giambrone's primary paper **Toward a Science of Other Minds: Escaping the Argument by Analogy** reports that comparative psychology historically assumed that human introspection reliably connected mental states to behavior and that similar behavior in other species had similar psychological causes. The authors argue that this analogy is flawed for second-order mental states, using chimpanzee understanding of visual attention as a test case. Their reinterpretation model proposes that shared social behavior can precede the psychological means of interpreting behavior in mentalistic terms.

Source: Povinelli, D. J., Bering, J. M., & Giambrone, S. **Toward a Science of Other Minds: Escaping the Argument by Analogy**, *Cognitive Science* 24(3), 509–541 (2000). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1207/S15516709COG2403_7. Wiley page: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15516709cog2403_7

Method implication for AION: behavioral similarity, learning curves, linguistic interaction or socially legible outputs are not sufficient by themselves to infer the same underlying mental-state representation. Cross-substrate transfer requires explicit disanalogy analysis, mechanistic or causal support where available, positive/negative controls and competing explanations.

## Theory of Mind: false-belief paradigm and LLM task evidence

Wimmer and Perner's primary study **Beliefs about beliefs: Representation and constraining function of wrong beliefs in young children's understanding of deception** introduced a false-belief paradigm in which understanding another person's wrong belief requires representing that belief as wrong relative to reality and the observer's knowledge. This is a paradigm for testing a bounded belief-attribution competence; it is not a direct test of the target's phenomenology and does not by itself establish consciousness or subjectivity.

Source: Wimmer, H., & Perner, J. **Beliefs about beliefs: Representation and constraining function of wrong beliefs in young children's understanding of deception**, *Cognition* 13(1), 103–128 (1983). PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6681741/ . DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-0277(83)90004-5

Kosinski's primary LLM study **Evaluating Large Language Models in Theory of Mind Tasks** assessed eleven LLMs with a custom battery of 640 prompts across 40 false-belief tasks, including matched true-belief controls and reversed versions. The arXiv record reports performance differences by model/version and frames the result as a possible ToM-like ability or possible by-product of improving language skills; the result is behavioral task performance, not proof of an internal mental-state representation, subjectivity or consciousness.

Source: Kosinski, M. **Evaluating Large Language Models in Theory of Mind Tasks**, arXiv:2302.02083v7 (2024; related PNAS DOI 10.1073/pnas.2405460121). https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02083

Method implication for AION: ToM task accuracy must be recorded as a capability/evidence-channel result with matched controls, prompt perturbations, transfer tests and alternative explanations. It cannot be promoted from `CAPABILITY` to `SCIENTIFIC_CONCLUSION` without independent mechanistic/causal/theory-derived evidence and cross-context robustness.

## Explanation, intervention and controls

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry **Causal Approaches to Scientific Explanation** surveys mechanistic and interventionist approaches. It treats causal explanation as identifying relevant difference-making relationships and discusses interventions, counterfactuals, mechanistic organization and explanatory depth. For this milestone, causal/mechanistic evidence is a channel that can strengthen an explanation when a putative mechanism is manipulated or its components are linked to the target behavior under scope; it is not automatically evidence of phenomenology.

Source: Ross, L. & Woodward, J. **Causal Approaches to Scientific Explanation**, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (2023). https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/causal-explanation-science/

A methodological review on negative controls explains that negative controls can reveal suspected or unsuspected sources of bias and confounding when an exposure/outcome relationship is expected to be absent under the control design. The PMC page was protected by a reCAPTCHA in the browser, so the accessible search record is used only for the bounded methodological statement and the citation is retained for later audit.

Source: Lipsitch, M., Tchetgen Tchetgen, E. & Cohen, T. **Negative controls: a tool for detecting confounding and bias in observational studies**, *Epidemiology* 21(3), 383–388 (2010). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3053408/

Method implication for AION: every proposed evidence channel needs a positive control showing that the measurement can detect the intended bounded pattern and a negative control designed to preserve superficial cues while removing the target-relevant relation. A negative control weakens a proposed explanation when it produces the same signal, but neither control alone establishes subjectivity.

Access limitation: PMC presented a reCAPTCHA page. No claim requiring the inaccessible full text is used in the milestone artifacts.

## Cross-substrate consciousness theories and transfer risk

Butlin et al.'s 2025/2026 primary opinion paper **Identifying indicators of consciousness in AI systems** presents a theory-derived indicator method: derive indicators from existing or future neuroscientific theories and use them to inform credences about whether particular AI systems are conscious. The paper explicitly notes uncertainty about consciousness theories, possible hardware/substrate objections, and that many theories were developed from human and mammalian evidence, leaving extension to AI unclear. It frames indicators as a way to reduce uncertainty, not as a single decisive detector.

Source: Butlin, P. et al. **Identifying indicators of consciousness in AI systems**, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 30(6), 488–501 (online 2025; issue 2026). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2025.10.011

Anil Seth's accepted 2025 manuscript **Conscious artificial intelligence and biological naturalism** argues for biological naturalism, challenges the assumption that computation is sufficient for consciousness, and treats consciousness as dependent on living-organism nature; it concludes that real artificial consciousness is unlikely on current trajectories but more plausible as systems become more brain-like and/or life-like. This is a competing theoretical position, not a settled premise for the milestone.

Source: Seth, A. K. **Conscious artificial intelligence and biological naturalism**, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, accepted manuscript published online 21 April 2025. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X25000032

Method implication for AION: a cross-substrate inference must register theory dependence and substrate-transfer assumptions explicitly. Functional or behavioral similarity may be evidence under a computational-functionalist model, while biological-naturalist or other substrate-sensitive models may treat the same evidence as insufficient. The method must therefore support competing theory rows, disanalogy penalties, alternative explanations and credence intervals; it must not silently assume substrate independence.

Source: Pargetter, R. **The scientific inference to other minds**, *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 62(2), 158–163 (1984). DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/00048408412341341. Method implication for AION: the retained method lead motivates representing IBE as a comparison among explicit hypotheses, while the framework must record alternatives, disanalogies and what would falsify or lower each explanation.
