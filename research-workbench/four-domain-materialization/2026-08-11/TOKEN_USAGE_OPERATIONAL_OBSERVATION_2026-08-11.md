## 3. Bounded research relevance

A research question raised from this observation is whether **long-horizon, provenance-sensitive, tool-using collaboration under finite computational resources** can incur substantial token-processing cost when prior state, repository context, correction history, and relevant memory must repeatedly be reconstructed.

A second question is whether selective, provenance-aware retrieval can reduce such cost while preserving the information needed for continuity-sensitive work.

These questions are compatible with the existing selective-memory/continuity control work in [`MEMORY_CONTINUITY_SELECTIVE_CONTROL_2026-08-11.md`](MEMORY_CONTINUITY_SELECTIVE_CONTROL_2026-08-11.md), but the present observation does not validate that mechanism.

### Candidate hypothesis, not a conclusion

```text
HYPOTHESIS_CANDIDATE:
Repeated reconstruction of large historical/repository context may contribute to high token-processing cost in long-horizon research workflows.

ALTERNATIVE_EXPLANATIONS:
- unrelated high-volume tasks;
- cached-input accounting;
- repeated tool or repository operations;
- model/task-specific accounting behavior;
- multiple sessions or agents;
- other usage not attributable to continuity maintenance.
```

Because the available screenshot provides no breakdown, all of these explanations remain open.

## 4. Future measurement protocol candidate

If sufficiently granular telemetry becomes available, a future controlled observation should separate, where available:

1. input, cached-input, and output token counts;
2. number and duration of sessions/tasks;
3. approximate context/retrieval volume without storing private conversation text;
4. repository/tool-call intensity;
5. task type and model/version;
6. use of selective recall versus broad context reconstruction;
7. matched-task resource cost before and after a retrieval-control intervention.

The protocol should prefer aggregate counters and synthetic or consented fixtures. Raw private conversations, credentials, account identifiers, and unrelated personal records should not be committed as telemetry evidence.

### Falsification / weakening conditions

The context-reconstruction hypothesis would be weakened if, under matched tasks:

- similarly high token usage occurs without substantial historical/context retrieval;
- selective retrieval does not reduce processing cost;
- the observed total is primarily explained by unrelated accounting categories or tasks.
