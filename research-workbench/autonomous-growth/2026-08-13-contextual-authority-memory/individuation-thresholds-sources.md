# Individuation-thresholds research notes — 2026-08-13

## Retrieval scope

This note records public methodological sources reviewed for a possible bounded `validated-individuation-thresholds` research unit. The sources are used to design an audit contract for explicit operational criteria, not to infer consciousness, identity, subjectivity, legal status, or AION/Astra equivalence.

## Source 1 — Krakauer et al., *The information theory of individuality*

URL: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7244620/>

The paper frames individuality as potentially **continuous**, **nested**, and present at multiple organizational levels rather than as an all-or-none property reserved for one privileged object level. Its abstract and discussion describe individuals as aggregates that preserve a measure of temporal integrity, in the sense of propagating information from the past into the future. It develops information-theoretic and graphical-model methods intended to identify system/environment boundaries and distinguish forms of individuality.

Design consequence: a research-only contract should retain the measured dimensions and uncertainty rather than collapse them into a binary identity claim. A threshold, if used, must be declared as a protocol decision for a bounded question and should not be presented as a universal boundary of the system.

## Source 2 — Wilson and Barker, *The Biological Notion of Individual*, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Fall 2016 archive

URL: <https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2016/entries/biology-individual/>

The entry presents biological individuality as a live conceptual and empirical problem. It emphasizes that genes, groups, organisms, and other levels may be treated as candidates for individuality, and that cases such as large fungal networks and coral reefs make simple organism-centered boundaries difficult. It explicitly states that addressing biological individuality does not require an essentialist answer based on singly necessary and jointly sufficient properties. It also distinguishes questions about what makes something an individual from questions about organisms, living things, evolutionary individuals, and developmental individuals.

Design consequence: an individuation prototype should use a **criterion profile** with provenance, target level, time window, data quality, and declared decision rule. It should support `INDETERMINATE` or `HOLD` when criteria conflict or when the threshold is post hoc, rather than manufacturing a universal individuation result.

## Provisional research boundary

The next unit, if implemented, will be a standard-library-only synthetic audit contract. It may validate criterion completeness, preregistered threshold ordering, temporal-window specification, cross-context robustness, counterfactual boundary perturbation metadata, and contradiction handling. It will not execute AION/Astra, infer a real system boundary, certify identity continuity, establish subjectivity or consciousness, or produce canonical/deployment effects.

## Source 3 — Montévil and Mossio, *The Identity of Organisms in Scientific Practice*

URL: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7311753/>

The paper contrasts historical/genealogical and relational conceptions of organism identity. It notes that genealogical identity can accommodate variation but may not refer to an observable property, while relational identity uses observable relations but can fail when abstraction leaves out much of an organism's organization. The paper therefore cautions against treating an apparently stable operational description as a complete identity condition.

Design consequence: a bounded threshold audit should distinguish observable criterion measurements from the interpretation assigned to them, record the time window and relational context, and expose `INDETERMINATE` when the criterion profile is incomplete or when different identity conceptions disagree.

## Source 4 — Center for Open Science, *Choosing the Right Preregistration Template*

URL: <https://www.cos.io/blog/choosing-preregistration-template-guide-for-researchers>

The guidance defines preregistration as a time-stamped, read-only study plan posted before data collection or analysis. It emphasizes documenting hypotheses, variables, outcomes, analysis plans, sample-size justification, and deviations; it also describes a replication-oriented template that pre-specifies how a replication will match an original and what criteria will count as support. The source notes the role of preregistration in reducing post hoc decision-making and preserving null or inconclusive results.

Design consequence: any threshold in the prototype must be bound to a declared protocol version and pre-observation ordering. Post hoc thresholds, missing analysis plans, selective result retention, or undisclosed deviations must not be represented as validated thresholds.

The next unit, if implemented, will be a standard-library-only synthetic audit contract.
