### 3. Sensorimotor research bridge

Human body-ownership experiments motivate attention to multisensory congruence rather than static visual resemblance alone. Robotics self-modeling work motivates testing morphology change together with action/sensation relationships. These findings are **research analogies and experimental inspirations**, not evidence that an artificial agent has human body ownership or phenomenology.

## External evidence crosswalk

### Botvinick & Cohen (1998), Nature

Rubber-hand illusion results linked bodily self-identification effects to interaction among vision, touch, and proprioception. Research use here: static geometry alone is an insufficient analogue for body ownership.

DOI: `10.1038/35784`

### Petkova & Ehrsson (2008), PLOS ONE

Full-body ownership illusions were induced through manipulated first-person perspective together with correlated multisensory information. Research use here: test embodied binding as a relation among morphology, perspective, and synchronized signals rather than visual mesh identity.

DOI: `10.1371/journal.pone.0003832`

### Bongard, Zykov & Lipson (2006), Science

A robot continuously inferred a self-model from actuation/sensation relationships and adapted after structural damage. Research use here: morphology can change while an engineering system preserves traceable adaptation and self-model update processes.

DOI: `10.1126/science.1133687`
