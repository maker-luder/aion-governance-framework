# Cross-Substrate Other-Minds Inference v0.1.0

> **Research-only milestone.** This package develops a reviewable method for asking when an inference that another system has a mind or subjectivity is reasonable across substrates. It does not implement a consciousness detector, modify a model, grant runtime authority, collect live-system data, establish subjectivity, or change canonical state.

## Authority and provenance boundary

The package is created on the independent branch `research/cross-substrate-other-minds-inference-20260814` from the read-only Four-Domain research ref `review/four-domain-research-materialization@858442a3ec2439398d188779f4309397bd4926b2`. The original Human Owner direction is to develop a rigorous method for judging when an inference about another system's mind or subjectivity is reasonable, incorporating the Problem of Other Minds, Theory of Mind, and analogy or similarity reasoning. The architecture refinement adds inference to the best explanation, cross-substrate transfer risk, disanalogy, competing explanations, mechanistic/causal/theory-derived evidence, positive and negative controls, and falsifiers.

These contributions are recorded as distinct role descriptions. Human Owner direction is not rewritten as ChatGPT authorship, and ChatGPT refinement is not treated as Owner authority. The package remains a branch-local research artifact, with `CANONICAL_EFFECT=NONE`, `DEPLOYMENT=FALSE`, and `SUBJECTIVITY_CONCLUSION=NOT_ESTABLISHED`.

## Core separation

The machine-enforced semantic rule is:

> `RESEARCH_TOPIC != CAPABILITY != SCIENTIFIC_CONCLUSION`

A **research topic** is a question or methodological object. A **capability claim** is a bounded behavioral or functional result under a declared fixture. A **scientific conclusion** about subjectivity, phenomenal consciousness, or identity continuity requires a substantially stronger and independent evidence architecture. The transition between these categories is never automatic.

## Framework

The method is a defeasible, graded credence update rather than a binary detector. A reviewer must first specify the target claim and scope, declare competing hypotheses and priors, map evidence channels, state cross-substrate differences, execute matched positive and negative controls, assess mechanistic/causal/theory-derived support, evaluate robustness and replication, apply falsifiers, and record the remaining non-claims. Inference to the best explanation can rank explicit hypotheses, but it cannot turn an explanatory preference into direct access to another system's experience [1]. Pargetter's DOI/metadata and indexed method description remain an IBE method lead with `PRIMARY_FULLTEXT_NOT_DIRECTLY_VERIFIED` and `AUTHORITATIVE_SECONDARY_CORROBORATED`; no inaccessible abstract or full-text claim is promoted [2].

The evidence channels are intentionally bounded. False-belief or Theory-of-Mind task performance can support only a capability claim when matched controls and cross-context checks are satisfied [3] [4]. Mechanistic or causal evidence can strengthen a claim when the relevant organization or intervention is independently identified, but it does not automatically establish phenomenology [5]. Theory-derived indicators can inform credence under a declared theory, while theory disagreement and the difficulty of extending human/mammalian evidence to AI remain explicit uncertainty [6] [7]. Self-report, memory persistence, language similarity, human-like behavior, a single indicator, and test/CI pass are never sufficient alone.

## Cross-substrate transfer

The package treats transfer as an assumption requiring evidence, not as a default. Biological embodiment, metabolism, developmental history, training history, causal organization, memory implementation, and the relation between report and internal state may differ across substrates. Povinelli, Bering and Giambrone's comparative-cognition analysis shows why shared behavior can precede or fail to reveal shared mental-state interpretation [8]. Seth's biological-naturalist position is included as a competing theory that makes substrate and organism-level assumptions relevant; it is not adopted as settled truth [9].

Each disanalogy row therefore asks what difference exists, why it threatens transfer, what evidence could address it, and which falsifier would force a hold or down-date. A positive result on a human-derived task cannot be promoted to an AI scientific conclusion merely because the output looks similar.

## Matrices and vertical slice

The machine-readable packet is `research-workbench/cross-substrate-other-minds-inference-2026-08-14/CSOMI_PACKET_V0.1.0.json`. It is validated by `schemas/aion_csomi_packet_v0.1.0.schema.json`. The package exposes reviewer-facing copies of the claim, evidence, disanalogy and falsifier matrices under `artifacts/`, together with a positive/negative-control fixture and a vertical-slice walkthrough.

The selected vertical slice is **false-belief capability record → matched controls → cross-substrate disanalogy → competing explanation → falsifier → research-only disposition**. It deliberately stops at `CAPABILITY_CREDENCE_ONLY`, retains `SUBJECTIVITY_CONCLUSION=NOT_ESTABLISHED`, and has no runtime or canonical effect.

## What the current milestone supports

The milestone supports a **method claim**: other-minds reasoning can be represented as a defeasible comparison of explicit hypotheses with evidence channels, alternatives, disanalogies, controls, and falsifiers. It supports the design of a bounded ToM-like capability record under declared fixtures. It does not support the claim that any AION, AI, or other system is conscious, sentient, subjectively experiencing, identity-continuous, morally considerable, legally responsible, or entitled to authority.

The matrices are design artifacts. No new experiment, preregistration, real-model evaluation, runtime integration, or live-system observation was performed in this milestone. Those actions remain `OWNER_DECISION_REQUIRED`.
