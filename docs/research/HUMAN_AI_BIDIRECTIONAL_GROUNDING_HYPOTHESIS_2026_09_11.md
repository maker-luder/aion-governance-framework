## 2. Human Owner original observation

The Human Owner proposed that a person does not merely receive explanations. In
repeated interactions the person may also correct the interaction state visible to
the AI by specifying:

- which layer the question actually concerns;
- which rules must govern the answer;
- which premises must not be silently introduced;
- what the user currently understands and still does not understand;
- which evidence cannot be promoted into proof;
- which earlier inference was corrected or rejected;
- which role and constraints the AI should follow; and
- which provenance distinctions and rejected branches must remain available.

The Human Owner further proposed that shared interaction history, explicit rules,
personalization, persistent memory or summaries, and repeated mutual correction
may affect later joint learning and problem decomposition.

`CO_CONSTRUCTED_THINKING_SPACE` names this possible interaction-level phenomenon
locally. It is not a claim that cognition, agency, experience or consciousness is
shared.

## 3. Candidate correspondence to prior work

Clark and Brennan's account of grounding treats communication as coordinated work
that updates common ground. Roschelle and Teasley use `joint problem space` for a
socially negotiated problem representation in human collaborative problem solving.
These are candidate analytical correspondences, not evidence that a human–AI dyad
has symmetric mental states. OpenAI's product documentation distinguishes saved
memory from referenced chat history; this establishes configurable retrieval
features, not subjective remembering or learning.

| External term or system feature | Permitted use here | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|
| Common ground / grounding | Describe repair and coordination demands | Mutual belief or symmetric understanding established |
| Scaffolding | Candidate description of adjusted explanation support | Durable human learning caused by the AI |
| Joint problem space | Candidate measure of stable task representation | Shared consciousness or co-agency |
| Persistent memory / chat-history reference | Experimental continuity condition | Subjective remembering or identity continuity |
| Personalization | Controlled rule/profile condition | Model-parameter learning or self-modification |
| Relational continuity | Functional interaction pattern | Phenomenal or personal identity continuity |

## 4. Core hypothesis H1

```text

MAY REDUCE RE_GROUNDING_COST

A_MORE_STABLE_LONGITUDINAL_JOINT_PROBLEM_REPRESENTATION
```

This is a conditional, comparative hypothesis. It does not state that memory
causes learning, personalization creates cognition, or a joint problem space proves
co-agency.

Candidate outcome measures include:

- concept continuity and terminology precision;
- provenance accuracy and confidence calibration;
- error detection and correction;
- re-grounding cost and branch continuity;
- held-out transfer and independent performance without AI support; and
- dependency, cognitive offloading and false-confidence costs.

## 5. Bidirectional candidate functions

### AI to human

- adjust explanations to reported knowledge state;
- scaffold and decompose concepts;
- provide corrective feedback and retrieval cues; and
- check whether grounding succeeded.

### Human to AI-visible interaction state

- clarify the intended question and correct mistaken assumptions;
- specify epistemic rules and report misunderstanding;
- reject unsupported inference;
- define interaction roles and constraints;
- correct the task representation;
- preserve provenance boundaries; and
- identify branches already rejected.

## 6. Replication boundary

`EXACT_DYAD_REPLICATION = NOT_EXPECTED`. The full interaction depends on human
traits, AI configuration, interaction history, personalization, memory or summaries,
shared terminology, correction history and provider infrastructure. This makes an
identical replay an inappropriate primary standard.

`MECHANISM_LEVEL_PARTIAL_REPLICATION = TESTABLE`. Candidate controlled contrasts
include:

1. history present versus absent;
2. personalization present versus absent;
3. persistent memory present versus absent;
4. provenance rules present versus absent;
5. complete versus truncated summary; and
6. fresh context versus longitudinal context.

The conditions should control task exposure, base model/version, tool access,
scoring procedure and time budget where possible. Held-out tasks and independent
scoring are needed to distinguish retained task structure from answer retrieval.

```text

!= INTERACTION_MECHANISM_UNTESTABLE
```

## 8. Candidate study design

A minimal longitudinal study could assign comparable problem sequences across a
factorial subset of the continuity conditions above. Each session would record
the supplied context, retrieved artifacts, explicit human corrections, AI outputs,
branch decisions, citations and correction receipts. Evaluation would separate:

- in-context reconstruction from cross-session continuity;
- task success from provenance accuracy;
- assisted performance from later independent performance;
- familiarity from held-out transfer; and
- useful personalization from dependence or confidence inflation.

The study should pre-register operational definitions for re-grounding cost,
continuity and transfer. Human-subject review, consent and privacy controls apply
where participant data are collected. Raw private transcripts and third-party
identities are not required for this repository artifact.

## 9. Falsifiers

H1 should be weakened or rejected if, under suitable controls:

- memory or personalization has no reproducible advantage after equal exposure;
- apparent gains disappear on held-out transfer;
- performance fails when AI support is removed;
- a fresh context fully reconstructs the same problem representation, provenance
  distinctions and branch path at comparable cost;
- persistent personalization increases only dependency or false confidence;
- current-context retrieval fully explains the effect;
- generic model capability improvement explains the observed difference; or
- human adaptation alone explains the effect.

## 10. Competing explanations

- simple retrieval of prior text rather than stable problem representation;
- prompt length, recency or lexical overlap;
- generic improvement in the underlying model;
- human practice, selection or expectancy effects;
- evaluator familiarity with preferred terminology;
- increased confidence without increased accuracy;
- dependency or cognitive offloading; and
- summary quality rather than longitudinal history as such.
