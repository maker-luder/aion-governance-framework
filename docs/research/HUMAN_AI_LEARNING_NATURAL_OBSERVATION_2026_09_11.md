## 1. Natural observation

The Human Owner reported helping a peer choose a computer for image-editing and possible local generative-AI assistance. During the discussion, the peer repeatedly asked for clarification of terms such as local execution, cloud computing, API, token, agent, compute and natural-language interaction.

The Human Owner had previously expected that a frequent ChatGPT user, including a paid-plan user who routinely asks questions and generates images, would likely possess at least a basic mental model of these concepts. The reported interaction violated that expectation.

A later conversation with another peer provided a small follow-up observation: after discussing the first interaction with ChatGPT, the Human Owner changed explanation strategy, answered closer to the listener's current question level, and stopped expanding every technical dependency at once. This is a reported transfer event, not evidence of a stable learning trait or causal effect.

## 2. Candidate interpretation: common-ground mismatch

A useful candidate explanation is that the participants did not share the same technical common ground. Terms that had become compressed, routine knowledge for one participant remained separate, unfamiliar elements for the other.

This suggests a communication failure mode:

```text
SPEAKER_COMPRESSED_SCHEMA
+
UNTESTED_LISTENER_PRIOR
->
JARGON_CHAIN
->
GROUNDING_REPAIR_REQUESTS
```

The repeated clarification questions can therefore be treated as candidate grounding-repair signals rather than evidence of low intelligence, lack of effort, or deception.

The repository should not infer that the peer was pretending not to understand. The simpler interpretation currently supported by the reported interaction is a genuine knowledge-gap hypothesis, while alternative explanations remain possible.

## 5. Important correction: usable knowledge is broader than immediate action

The Human Owner proposed an epistemic preference: information that cannot be used risks remaining "only text." That preference can be retained as a source-attributed statement, but it should not become a universal definition of knowledge.

For this research note, `USE` is broader than immediate task execution:

```text
ACTION_USE
EXPLANATORY_USE
PREDICTIVE_USE
DISCRIMINATIVE_USE
FALSIFICATION_USE
STRUCTURAL_USE
```

A concept may therefore be useful even when it does not immediately produce an action, if it improves explanation, prediction, discrimination, falsification or the structure of later learning.

## 9. Research questions extracted from the event

The event motivates, but does not answer, the following questions:

1. Under the same generative-AI product access, how strongly do different interaction patterns predict differences in users' technical mental models?
2. Does iterative co-inquiry produce different knowledge structures from primarily task-oriented AI use?
3. Can a learner acquire systems-level relational fluency before fully precise definitions of component concepts?
4. Does repeated model revision improve transfer to novel communication or technical tasks?
5. How quickly can a newly revised rule become subjectively "normal," and is that normalization associated with successful transfer or merely familiarity?
6. How accurately can a learner estimate another user's common ground after long exposure to a specialized information environment?
7. Which outcomes distinguish learning from dependence, cognitive offloading or answer accumulation?

## 10. Stronger evidence required

A stronger claim about human–AI co-development would require more than this event. Candidate future evidence includes:

- pre-registered longitudinal tasks;
- baseline measures of domain knowledge;
- repeated held-out transfer tasks;
- blinded scoring of explanation accuracy and terminology;
- comparison between task-oriented AI use and iterative co-inquiry conditions;
- measures of independent performance without AI assistance;
- explicit checks for cognitive offloading and source-blind acceptance;
- replication with multiple participants and different AI systems;
- human-subject research review where applicable.
