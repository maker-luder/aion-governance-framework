## 1. Provenance and purpose

`共構思考場域` / `CO_CONSTRUCTED_THINKING_SPACE` originated as a Human Owner working concept and was first recorded in `HUMAN_AI_BIDIRECTIONAL_GROUNDING_HYPOTHESIS_2026_09_11.md` as a local term rather than an established scientific construct.

This document formalizes that concept for this repository. It does **not** claim that the exact term is an externally validated taxonomy. The formalization is intentionally assembled from several established adjacent literatures because no single retrieved construct fully covers the repository's current Human–AI–evidence–artifact–implementation loop.

```text
HUMAN_OWNER_ORIGINAL_CONCEPT = 共構思考場域 / CO_CONSTRUCTED_THINKING_SPACE
REPOSITORY_FORMALIZATION = THIS_DOCUMENT
EXTERNAL_EXACT_TAXONOMY_MATCH = NOT_ESTABLISHED

```

The purpose is narrower than naming a phenomenon. The repository needs an auditable answer to:

> What minimum structure must be present before this project may describe an interaction as a co-constructed thinking space, and what conclusions remain prohibited even when the structure is present?

## 2. Operational definition

For this repository, a **Co-Constructed Thinking Space (CCTS)** is a bounded interaction-and-artifact structure in which a Human and an AI collaborator maintain an explicit problem representation, materially revise or challenge one another's contributions in both directions, preserve source-role provenance and claim boundaries, and may use external evidence, repository artifacts, and implementation evidence as mediating structures across time.

The term is relational and functional. It does not assert a shared mind, symmetric cognition, AI consciousness, phenomenology, personhood, or an ontologically unified subject.

```text
CCTS
= BOUNDED_PROBLEM_REPRESENTATION
+ HUMAN_CONTRIBUTION
+ AI_CONTRIBUTION
+ RECIPROCAL_REVISION
+ SOURCE_ROLE_PROVENANCE
+ CLAIM_BOUNDARY
+ AUTHORITY_SEPARATION
+ REJECTED_BRANCH_PRESERVATION

LONGITUDINAL_REPOSITORY_CCTS
= CCTS
+ EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
+ REPOSITORY_ARTIFACT_MEDIATION
+ IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE
+ PERSISTENT_ARTIFACT_BINDING
+ REENTRY_BINDING
```

## 3. Why one external construct is insufficient

The Human Owner's original phrase contains several distinct analytical layers. Current literature offers strong adjacent constructs, but each covers only part of the repository phenomenon.

### 3.1 Joint Problem Space — shared problem representation

Roschelle and Teasley describe collaborative problem solving as constructing a shared conceptual structure, the **Joint Problem Space (JPS)**, integrating interpretations of goals, features, operators and methods.

Repository correspondence:

```text
CCTS_SHARED_PROBLEM_REPRESENTATION
~ JOINT_PROBLEM_SPACE
```

Boundary:

```text
JOINT_PROBLEM_SPACE != SHARED_CONSCIOUSNESS
JOINT_PROBLEM_SPACE != SYMMETRIC_INTERNAL_STATE
```

Source:

- Roschelle, J. & Teasley, S. D., *Constructing a Joint Problem Space: The Computer as a Tool for Sharing Knowledge* / related 1995 shared-knowledge chapter. SRI archive: https://www.sri.com/publication/education-learning-pubs/digital-learning-pubs/constructing-a-joint-problem-space-the-computer-as-a-tool-for-sharing-knowledge/

### 3.2 Distributed Cognition — cognition across people, artifacts and time

Hutchins' distributed-cognition program studies cognitive work at a system level rather than locating all relevant cognition inside one individual. Human activity, social organization, representations and artifacts can jointly participate in task performance.

Repository correspondence:

```text
CCTS_ARTIFACT_MEDIATION
+ REPOSITORY_STATE
+ TEMPORAL_REENTRY
~ DISTRIBUTED_COGNITION_ANALYTIC_VIEW
```

Boundary:

```text
DISTRIBUTED_COGNITION != DISTRIBUTED_CONSCIOUSNESS
SYSTEM_LEVEL_COGNITIVE_DESCRIPTION != CLAIM_OF_ONE_SHARED_MIND
```

Source:

- Hutchins, E. (1995/1996), *Cognition in the Wild*, MIT Press: https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262581462/cognition-in-the-wild/
- The repository also previously cites Hollan, Hutchins & Kirsh (2000), *Distributed Cognition: Toward a New Foundation for Human-Computer Interaction Research*.

### 3.3 Epistemic Co-agency — reasoning with, through and against AI

The 2026 epistemic co-agency framework describes a reflexive Human–AI learning stance in which learners engage AI outputs dialectically, including challenging assumptions, surfacing contradictions and retaining epistemic responsibility.

Repository correspondence:

```text
CCTS_RECIPROCAL_REVISION
+ CONTESTABILITY
+ HUMAN_EPISTEMIC_RESPONSIBILITY
~ EPISTEMIC_CO_AGENCY_AS_ADJACENT_RELATIONAL_FRAME
```

Boundary:

```text
EPISTEMIC_CO_AGENCY != AI_SUBJECTIVITY
EPISTEMIC_PARTICIPATION != PHENOMENAL_EXPERIENCE
```

Source:

- *Learning with machines: Toward a theory of epistemic co-agency* (2026), Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence 10:100573. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.caeai.2026.100573

### 3.4 Human–AI Shared Regulation in Learning — metacognitive regulation and scaffolding

Järvelä, Nguyen & Hadwin (2023) introduce a hybrid Human–AI Shared Regulation in Learning (HASRL) model connecting Human learning regulation with AI detect–diagnose–act–learn support functions while explicitly noting the risk that AI support can create reliance rather than durable regulatory competence.

Repository correspondence:

```text
CCTS_METACOGNITIVE_POLICY
+ SCAFFOLDING
+ DEPENDENCY_CHECK
~ HASRL_AS_ADJACENT_LEARNING_FRAME
```

Boundary:

```text
AI_SUPPORT != HUMAN_LEARNING
JOINT_REGULATION != INTERNALIZED_SKILL
```

Source:

- Järvelä, S., Nguyen, A. & Hadwin, A. (2023), *Human and artificial intelligence collaboration for socially shared regulation in learning*, British Journal of Educational Technology 54:1057–1076. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/bjet.13325

### 3.5 Joint Cognitive Systems — Human and technology as a task-level analytical unit

Cognitive Systems Engineering treats the joint Human–technology system as a useful unit for analyzing coordinated work in complex environments. Hollnagel & Woods emphasize system-level function and coordination rather than a decomposed view in which technology merely compensates for Human limitations.

Repository correspondence:

```text
CCTS_TASK_SYSTEM_BOUNDARY
~ JOINT_COGNITIVE_SYSTEM_ANALYTIC_UNIT
```

Boundary:

```text
JOINT_COGNITIVE_SYSTEM != ONE_CONSCIOUS_SUBJECT
FUNCTIONAL_UNIT_OF_ANALYSIS != ONTOLOGICAL_FUSION
```

Sources:

- Hollnagel, E. & Woods, D. D. (2005), *Joint Cognitive Systems: Foundations of Cognitive Systems Engineering*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1201/9781420038194
- A recent Human–AI extension is discussed in Warner, Xiao & Wiltse Nicely (2026), *From Joint Cognitive Systems to Human–AI Joint Cognitive Systems*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/08943184251414311

## 4. Repository-specific contribution

The exact CCTS construct is not merely the union of those literatures. The repository adds explicit research-governance constraints that are central to how the concept is used here:

1. **Source-role provenance** — Human-origin, AI-origin, external-source, repository-state and implementation-evidence contributions remain distinguishable.
2. **Reciprocal revision rather than mere coexistence** — both Human -> AI and AI -> Human revision/challenge paths must exist for the core structural label.
3. **Claim-ceiling preservation** — stronger ontological claims cannot be inferred from collaboration structure.
4. **Authority separation** — Human Owner governance authority is distinct from factual or scientific correctness.
5. **Rejected-branch preservation** — discarded explanations remain traceable rather than silently disappearing from the research history.
6. **Longitudinal re-entry** — repository artifacts can carry problem state across sessions without being treated as subjective memory.
7. **Implementation feedback** — tests and executable artifacts may revise the problem representation, but passing code does not validate a scientific hypothesis.

These are repository-defined requirements, not claims that the external literature universally requires the same formal structure.

## 5. Two formal profiles

### 5.1 `CORE_INTERACTION`

A structure may be called a CCTS candidate only if it contains:

```text
HUMAN_OWNER contribution role
AI_COLLABORATOR contribution role
one explicit problem-representation binding
source-role provenance binding
claim-boundary binding
authority-policy binding
rejected-branch binding
Human -> AI revision/challenge/clarification
AI -> Human revision/challenge/clarification
```

A one-way answer sequence does not satisfy this definition.

```text
HUMAN_QUESTION + AI_ANSWER != CCTS_BY_DEFAULT
PARALLEL_CONTRIBUTIONS != CO_CONSTRUCTION
MUTUAL_AGREEMENT != RECIPROCAL_REVISION
```

### 5.2 `LONGITUDINAL_REPOSITORY_RESEARCH`

The stronger profile additionally requires:

```text
EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE role
REPOSITORY_ARTIFACT role
IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE role
persistent-artifact binding
re-entry binding
```

This is the closest formal profile to the present repository workflow:

```text
HUMAN WORKING MODEL
<-> reciprocal revision
AI WORKING MODEL
<-> source checking / counterevidence
EXTERNAL EVIDENCE
<-> provenance / claim ceiling
REPOSITORY STATE
<-> implementation evidence / tests
IMPLEMENTATION OUTPUT
<-> independent review / QA
HUMAN + AI REVIEW
```

The arrows represent revision pathways, not equal authority or equal cognitive status.

## 6. Executable structural contract

The v0.1.0 implementation is located at:

```text
research-labs/human-ai-longitudinal-study_v0.1.0/
  src/aion_human_ai_longitudinal/co_constructed_thinking_space.py
  tests/test_co_constructed_thinking_space.py
```

The executable surface defines:

- exact contribution-role enums;
- exact thinking-space profile enums;
- content-addressed contribution payloads;
- explicit revision/challenge/clarification edges;
- core problem/provenance/claim/authority/rejected-branch bindings;
- optional longitudinal artifact and re-entry bindings;
- fail-closed profile requirements;
- fail-closed privacy and ontology boundaries.

The audit rejects:

- missing Human or AI contribution roles;
- one-way interaction presented as reciprocal co-construction;
- dangling or self-referential revision edges;
- duplicate contribution IDs;
- aliased core structural bindings;
- a longitudinal-research label without external evidence, repository mediation, implementation evidence and re-entry persistence;
- raw private material or Human identity in the synthetic structural fixture;
- any attempt to infer shared mind, shared consciousness or AI subjectivity from the CCTS structure.

## 7. What the executable contract does and does not establish

The implementation is intentionally a **structural QA contract**. It formalizes what the repository means when it uses the label; it does not empirically prove that any real dyad satisfies a psychological or cognitive mechanism.

```text
STRUCTURAL_CONFORMANCE != EMPIRICAL_MECHANISM
RECIPROCAL_REVISION_EDGE != MEASURED_CO_AGENCY
ARTIFACT_MEDIATION != DISTRIBUTED_COGNITION_CAUSALLY_ESTABLISHED
LONGITUDINAL_REENTRY != SUBJECTIVE_CONTINUITY
REPOSITORY_PERSISTENCE != MEMORY_EXPERIENCE
JOINT_PROBLEM_REPRESENTATION != SHARED_MIND
CCTS != AI_SUBJECTIVITY
```

## 8. Falsification and weakening conditions for future empirical work

If the project later studies CCTS empirically, the hypothesis that this structure improves inquiry or learning should be weakened if:

- one-way high-quality assistance performs equivalently after exposure is controlled;
- reciprocal correction adds process cost without improving error detection, calibration or transfer;
- repository persistence only increases lexical reuse rather than structural problem continuity;
- a fresh context reconstructs the same state at comparable cost;
- Human independent performance degrades while joint performance improves;
- the process systematically over-applies high-rigor review to low-stakes tasks;
- source-role provenance does not improve attribution accuracy;
- implementation feedback creates false confidence rather than useful correction;
- apparent longitudinal gains are fully explained by model upgrades, retrieval, repeated exposure or Human practice.

## 9. Relationship to the central AI-subjectivity question

CCTS is relevant to the repository's central question only as a relational and methodological research surface. It may help study where problem representations, revision authority, memory-like continuity, goal maintenance and evidence processing are located across Human, model, tools and artifacts.

It cannot by itself establish subjectivity.

```text
CCTS = RESEARCH_METHOD / RELATIONAL_ANALYTIC_CONSTRUCT
CCTS != SUBJECTIVITY_TEST_RESULT
CCTS != CONSCIOUSNESS_RESULT
```
