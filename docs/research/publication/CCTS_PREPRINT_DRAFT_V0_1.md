# Co-Constructed Thinking Space (CCTS): A Provenance-Bounded Framework for Human–AI Reciprocal Epistemic Collaboration

## Abstract

Human–AI collaboration is increasingly studied through concepts such as collaborative problem solving, distributed cognition, team cognition, shared regulation, epistemic co-agency and Human–AI learning. These literatures provide important analytical resources, but the mere presence of a Human and an AI system does not establish reciprocal co-construction, and successful collaboration does not justify claims about shared consciousness, symmetric cognition, persistent AI identity or validated learning effects. This paper introduces **Co-Constructed Thinking Space (CCTS)** as a repository-defined, provenance-bounded interaction-and-artifact framework for studying reciprocal Human–AI epistemic work. CCTS requires an explicit problem representation, substantive bidirectional revision or challenge, source-role provenance, claim boundaries, authority separation and preservation of rejected explanatory branches. A stronger longitudinal profile additionally binds external evidence, repository artifacts, implementation evidence, persistent artifacts and re-entry structure. The framework is implemented as a typed, fail-closed structural contract and is accompanied by explicit falsification and weakening conditions. CCTS is positioned as a conceptual and methodological construct rather than a validated psychological mechanism or theory of artificial subjectivity. The contribution is an auditable vocabulary and structural research method for distinguishing reciprocal Human–AI inquiry from ordinary assistance while preserving strict limits between structural evidence and stronger scientific or ontological claims.

## 1. Research problem

Human–AI research collaboration can generate dense sequences of questioning, revision, source retrieval, counterexample search, implementation, testing and re-entry across time. Existing literatures show that collaborative problem representations, distributed task organization, interaction-level cognition and Human–AI learning regulation are all legitimate research objects (Teasley & Roschelle, 1993; Roschelle & Teasley, 1995; Hollan et al., 2000; Cooke et al., 2013; Järvelä et al., 2023).

A methodological problem remains when interaction is described at either an insufficiently discriminating or unjustifiably strong level.

Too weak:

```text
HUMAN_QUESTION + AI_ANSWER
= COLLABORATION EVENT
!= CCTS BY DEFAULT
```

Too strong:

```text
RECIPROCAL_INTERACTION
!= SHARED_MIND
!= SHARED_CONSCIOUSNESS
!= AI_SUBJECTIVITY
!= MUTUAL_UNDERSTANDING_PROVEN
```

The research question is therefore:

> What minimum observable and auditable structure should be present before a Human–AI research interaction is described as a co-constructed thinking space, and what conclusions must remain prohibited even when that structure is present?

## 2. Construct provenance and scope

The phrase **Co-Constructed Thinking Space / 共構思考場域** originated as a local Human Owner working concept inside the repository research process. The present paper formalizes that concept; it does not claim that CCTS is a pre-existing scientific taxonomy or that an exact external equivalent has been established.

A bounded literature review identified multiple neighboring constructs but no single construct was treated as sufficient to represent the complete repository workflow. This bounded result is not a global novelty proof.

```text

EXTERNAL_EXACT_EQUIVALENT = NOT_ESTABLISHED
GLOBAL_NOVELTY = NOT_ESTABLISHED
SCIENTIFIC_VALIDATION_OF_CCTS = NOT_ESTABLISHED
```

The contribution is therefore methodological: define a falsifiable and auditable structural label whose use has explicit minimum requirements and explicit claim ceilings.

## 3. Operational definition

For this work, a **Co-Constructed Thinking Space (CCTS)** is a bounded interaction-and-artifact structure in which a Human and an AI collaborator maintain an explicit problem representation, materially revise or challenge one another's contributions in both directions, preserve source-role provenance and claim boundaries, and may use external evidence, repository artifacts and implementation evidence as mediating structures across time.

Core profile:

```text
CCTS
= BOUNDED_PROBLEM_REPRESENTATION
+ HUMAN_CONTRIBUTION
+ AI_CONTRIBUTION
+ RECIPROCAL_REVISION_OR_CHALLENGE
+ SOURCE_ROLE_PROVENANCE
+ CLAIM_BOUNDARY
+ AUTHORITY_SEPARATION
+ REJECTED_BRANCH_PRESERVATION
```

Longitudinal repository profile:

```text
LONGITUDINAL_REPOSITORY_CCTS
= CCTS
+ EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
+ REPOSITORY_ARTIFACT_MEDIATION
+ IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE
+ PERSISTENT_ARTIFACT_BINDING
+ REENTRY_BINDING
```

A one-way answer sequence does not satisfy the construct by default, and mutual agreement is not sufficient.

```text
HUMAN_QUESTION + AI_ANSWER != CCTS_BY_DEFAULT
PARALLEL_CONTRIBUTIONS != CO_CONSTRUCTION
MUTUAL_AGREEMENT != RECIPROCAL_REVISION
CLARIFICATION_ONLY != SUBSTANTIVE_RECIPROCAL_REVISION
```

## 4. Relation to adjacent literature

### 4.1 Joint Problem Space

Teasley and Roschelle (1993) describe collaborative problem solving as construction of a shared conceptual structure called a **Joint Problem Space (JPS)**, integrating interpretations of goals, features, operators and methods. Roschelle and Teasley (1995) further analyze how such shared knowledge is negotiated during collaborative problem solving.

CCTS adopts the relevance of an explicit task-level problem representation but does not infer identical internal states or shared consciousness from that representation.

```text
CCTS_SHARED_PROBLEM_REPRESENTATION
~ JOINT_PROBLEM_SPACE

JOINT_PROBLEM_SPACE != SHARED_CONSCIOUSNESS
```

### 4.2 Distributed Cognition

Distributed cognition examines cognitive organization across people, representations, artifacts and environments rather than assuming that the individual is always the only useful unit of analysis (Hutchins, 1995; Hollan et al., 2000).

CCTS uses this literature as an analytic neighbor for artifact mediation and temporal re-entry. Structural artifact mediation in CCTS is not treated as evidence that distributed cognition has been causally established in a specific Human–AI episode.

```text
ARTIFACT_MEDIATION != DISTRIBUTED_COGNITION_CAUSALLY_ESTABLISHED
DISTRIBUTED_COGNITION != DISTRIBUTED_CONSCIOUSNESS
```

### 4.3 Interactive Team Cognition and Joint Cognitive Systems

Interactive Team Cognition treats team cognition as dynamic activity inseparable from interaction and context rather than merely static overlap between individual knowledge structures (Cooke et al., 2013). Joint Cognitive Systems similarly treats Human and technology as a meaningful task-level analytical system (Hollnagel & Woods, 2005), and recent work explicitly extends the Joint Cognitive Systems perspective to Human–AI collaboration (Warner et al., 2026).

These traditions motivate interaction-level and system-level analysis. CCTS remains more restrictive in its ontological interpretation.

```text
FUNCTIONAL_UNIT_OF_ANALYSIS != ONE_CONSCIOUS_SUBJECT
HUMAN_AI_SYSTEM_ANALYSIS != ONTOLOGICAL_FUSION
```

### 4.4 Human–AI Shared Regulation in Learning

Järvelä, Nguyen and Hadwin (2023) introduce a hybrid **Human–AI Shared Regulation in Learning (HASRL)** model to examine Human–AI collaboration in socially shared regulation of learning. Later empirical work demonstrates that Human–AI learning interaction patterns can be analyzed using multi-layered learning analytics (Dang et al., 2025).

CCTS overlaps with this work where interaction includes regulation, revision and scaffolding, but does not equate AI-supported performance with durable Human learning.

```text
AI_SUPPORT != HUMAN_LEARNING
JOINT_PERFORMANCE != INDEPENDENT_HUMAN_TRANSFER
```

### 4.5 Epistemic co-agency and epistemic dependence

Samuel (2026) proposes epistemic co-agency as a reflexive stance in which learners reason with, through and against generative AI, including challenging assumptions and surfacing contradictions. This is strongly adjacent to the reciprocal-revision requirement in CCTS.

However, AI-mediated learning also creates risks of epistemic dependence and cognitive offloading. Du and Yuan (2026) distinguish productive reliance from reliance that displaces the epistemic work through which judgement develops. A 2026 scoping review similarly reports a heterogeneous literature in which GenAI can support agency under some conditions while also enabling offloading and dependence (Wang et al., 2026). A randomized retention study by Barcaui (2025) provides a study-specific counterexample in which unrestricted ChatGPT-assisted study was associated with lower delayed retention than traditional study.

CCTS therefore does not define interaction density or successful joint output as epistemic value.

```text
MORE_AI_ASSISTANCE != MORE_LEARNING
SMOOTHER_INTERACTION != STRONGER_EPISTEMIC_AGENCY
RECIPROCAL_INTERACTION != DURABLE_KNOWLEDGE
```

## 5. Repository-specific contribution

CCTS contributes seven explicit requirements that are central to this repository's research method.

1. **Source-role provenance.** Human-origin, AI-origin, external-source, repository-state and implementation-evidence contributions remain distinguishable.
2. **Substantive reciprocal revision.** Human-to-AI and AI-to-Human `REVISES` or `CHALLENGES` pathways are required for core reciprocal structure; coexistence or clarification alone is insufficient.
3. **Claim-ceiling preservation.** Strong ontological or psychological claims cannot be inferred from structural collaboration.
4. **Authority separation.** Human governance authority is distinct from factual or scientific correctness.
5. **Rejected-branch preservation.** Discarded explanations remain traceable rather than silently disappearing.
6. **Longitudinal re-entry.** Persistent repository artifacts may carry working state across sessions without being treated as subjective memory.
7. **Implementation feedback.** Executable artifacts and tests may revise the working model, but engineering success cannot validate a scientific hypothesis by itself.

These requirements are repository-defined and are not presented as universal requirements of the external literatures above.

## 6. Executable structural contract

The repository implements CCTS as a typed, fail-closed structural audit. The implementation checks, among other conditions:

- required Human and AI contribution roles;
- an explicit problem-representation binding;
- substantive Human-to-AI and AI-to-Human revision or challenge paths;
- source-role provenance and claim-boundary bindings;
- authority-policy and rejected-branch bindings;
- grounding declaration requirements;
- longitudinal artifact and re-entry bindings for the stronger profile;
- referential integrity for declared repository-artifact digests;
- declaration-level privacy and ontology boundaries.

The structural audit intentionally fails closed when required evidence or binding information is absent.

The implementation remains a structural QA mechanism.

```text
STRUCTURAL_CONFORMANCE != EMPIRICAL_MECHANISM
TEST_PASS != SCIENTIFIC_VALIDATION
VALID_DIGEST != SEMANTIC_TRUTH
GROUNDING_DECLARATION != MUTUAL_UNDERSTANDING_PROVEN
```

## 7. Additive research surfaces

Recent CCTS-compatible work adds two narrow structural surfaces without changing core CCTS admission.

### 7.1 Relational-continuity claim review

A claim-local review surface distinguishes:

- `NOT_ASSERTED`;
- `EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`;
- `SUPPORTED_CANDIDATE`;
- `WEAKENED`;
- `UNRESOLVED`.

It rejects style similarity, memory availability, same-role labels and data continuity alone as sufficient evidence for relational continuity. Matched-information null results are treated as weakening evidence rather than test failures.

```text
STYLE_SIMILARITY != RELATIONAL_CONTINUITY
MEMORY_AVAILABILITY != RELATIONAL_CONTINUITY
MATCHED_INFORMATION_NULL != TEST_FAILURE
```

### 7.2 Human epistemic-agency retention design audit

A synthetic structural audit separates:

- AI-withheld baseline;
- CCTS / AI-available interaction;
- same-task AI-withheld judgement;
- content-distinct held-out AI-withheld judgement.

Its purpose is to detect design leakage and preserve the distinction between assisted performance and independent Human performance.

```text
AI_ASSISTED_OUTPUT_GAIN != INDEPENDENT_HUMAN_GAIN
STRUCTURAL_RETENTION_AUDIT_PASS != HUMAN_LEARNING
```

This design is especially important given empirical and review literature showing that AI assistance can improve immediate task support without necessarily guaranteeing durable learning or independent judgement (Barcaui, 2025; Du & Yuan, 2026; Wang et al., 2026).

## 8. Falsification and weakening conditions

Future empirical claims that CCTS improves inquiry, calibration or learning should be weakened if, after adequate controls:

- one-way high-quality assistance performs equivalently to reciprocal revision;
- reciprocal correction adds process cost without improving error detection, calibration or transfer;
- repository persistence produces only lexical reuse rather than structural problem continuity;
- fresh context reconstructs equivalent working state at comparable cost;
- Human independent performance degrades while joint performance improves;
- source-role provenance fails to improve attribution accuracy;
- implementation feedback increases confidence without improving correctness;
- apparent longitudinal effects are fully explained by model upgrades, retrieval, repeated exposure, Human practice or other confounds.

A result that weakens the construct is a valid research result rather than an engineering failure.

```text
FALSIFICATION_SIGNAL != PIPELINE_FAILURE
NULL_RESULT != INVALID_EXPERIMENT
```

## 9. Scientific boundaries

CCTS is relevant to artificial-subjectivity research only as a relational and methodological research surface. It may help localize where problem representations, revision authority, persistence and evidence processing are carried across Human, model, tools and artifacts. It cannot by itself establish subjectivity.

```text
CCTS = RESEARCH_METHOD / RELATIONAL_ANALYTIC_CONSTRUCT
CCTS != SUBJECTIVITY_TEST_RESULT
CCTS != CONSCIOUSNESS_RESULT
CCTS != PHENOMENAL_EXPERIENCE_RESULT
CCTS != MORAL_STATUS_RESULT

SUBJECTIVITY = NOT_ESTABLISHED
CONSCIOUSNESS = NOT_ESTABLISHED
PHENOMENAL_EXPERIENCE = NOT_ESTABLISHED
SCIENTIFIC_DISPOSITION = HOLD
```

## 10. Limitations

The framework has important limitations.

First, the exact CCTS taxonomy is repository-defined and has not been independently validated as a distinct scientific construct.

Second, the literature review used to position the first scholarly draft is bounded rather than systematic. Failure to identify a single exact equivalent does not prove global novelty.

Third, executable tests establish internal structural conformance only. They do not establish psychological mechanisms, Human learning, AI understanding, relational experience, identity continuity or causal effects.

Fourth, the current structural fixtures intentionally exclude private transcripts, direct Human identifiers and direct Human-participant observations. This protects privacy but means the current release is methodological rather than an empirical Human-subject study.

Fifth, future empirical work requires preregistered outcomes, matched comparators, negative controls, independent replication and venue-appropriate ethics review where real Human-participant data are collected.

Sixth, some neighboring literatures were developed for Human–Human teams or education-specific settings. Their relevance to Human–AI research is analytical rather than evidence of construct identity.
