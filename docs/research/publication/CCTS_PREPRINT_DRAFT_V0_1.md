# Co-Constructed Thinking Space (CCTS): A Provenance-Bounded Framework for Human–AI Reciprocal Epistemic Collaboration

## Abstract

Human–AI collaboration is often described through broad labels such as assistance, co-agency, team cognition, shared regulation, or mutual learning. These labels can obscure an important methodological distinction: the presence of two contributors does not by itself establish reciprocal co-construction, and successful collaboration does not justify claims about shared consciousness, symmetric cognition, persistent AI identity, or validated learning effects. This paper introduces **Co-Constructed Thinking Space (CCTS)** as a repository-defined, provenance-bounded interaction-and-artifact framework for studying reciprocal Human–AI epistemic work. CCTS requires an explicit problem representation, substantive bidirectional revision or challenge, source-role provenance, claim boundaries, authority separation, and preservation of rejected explanatory branches. A stronger longitudinal profile additionally binds external evidence, repository artifacts, implementation evidence, persistent artifacts and re-entry structure. The framework is implemented as a fail-closed structural contract and is accompanied by falsification and weakening conditions. CCTS is positioned as a conceptual and methodological construct rather than a validated psychological mechanism or theory of artificial subjectivity. The contribution is intended to make Human–AI research interactions more auditable, contestable and reproducible while preserving strict limits between structural evidence and stronger scientific or ontological claims.

## 1. Research problem

Human–AI research collaboration can generate dense sequences of questioning, revision, evidence retrieval, implementation and re-entry across time. A methodological problem arises when these sequences are described using concepts that are either too weak or too strong.

Too weak:

```text
HUMAN_QUESTION + AI_ANSWER
= COLLABORATION EVENT
!= CCTS BY DEFAULT
```

Too strong:

```text
RECIPROCAL_INTERACTION
!= SHARED_MIND
!= SHARED_CONSCIOUSNESS
!= AI_SUBJECTIVITY
!= MUTUAL_UNDERSTANDING_PROVEN
```

The research question is therefore:

> What minimum observable and auditable structure should be present before a Human–AI research interaction is described as a co-constructed thinking space, and what conclusions must remain prohibited even when that structure is present?

## 2. Operational definition

For this work, a **Co-Constructed Thinking Space (CCTS)** is a bounded interaction-and-artifact structure in which a Human and an AI collaborator maintain an explicit problem representation, materially revise or challenge one another's contributions in both directions, preserve source-role provenance and claim boundaries, and may use external evidence, repository artifacts and implementation evidence as mediating structures across time.

Core profile:

```text
CCTS
= BOUNDED_PROBLEM_REPRESENTATION
+ HUMAN_CONTRIBUTION
+ AI_CONTRIBUTION
+ RECIPROCAL_REVISION_OR_CHALLENGE
+ SOURCE_ROLE_PROVENANCE
+ CLAIM_BOUNDARY
+ AUTHORITY_SEPARATION
+ REJECTED_BRANCH_PRESERVATION
```

Longitudinal repository profile:

```text
LONGITUDINAL_REPOSITORY_CCTS
= CCTS
+ EXTERNAL_EVIDENCE
+ REPOSITORY_ARTIFACT_MEDIATION
+ IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE
+ PERSISTENT_ARTIFACT_BINDING
+ REENTRY_BINDING
```

## 3. Relation to adjacent literature

CCTS is not presented as a pre-existing external taxonomy. It is a local formalization assembled against several adjacent research traditions.

### 3.1 Joint Problem Space

Joint Problem Space work provides an adjacent account of collaboratively maintained problem representations. CCTS adopts the relevance of shared task representation while explicitly rejecting any inference from shared representation to shared consciousness or identical internal states.

### 3.2 Distributed Cognition

Distributed-cognition research motivates attention to artifacts, representations, social organization and temporal structure as parts of task performance. CCTS uses this as an analytic neighbor, not as proof that distributed cognition is the causal mechanism operating in a given Human–AI episode.

### 3.3 Epistemic Co-agency

Epistemic co-agency provides a nearby account of reasoning with, through and against AI while preserving Human epistemic responsibility. CCTS adds repository-specific provenance, claim-ceiling and audit requirements.

### 3.4 Human–AI Shared Regulation in Learning

Shared-regulation work is relevant to scaffolding, metacognitive regulation and dependency risks. CCTS does not treat AI-supported performance as equivalent to durable Human learning.

### 3.5 Joint Cognitive Systems and team-cognition perspectives

Joint-system and team-cognition perspectives support analysis at the interaction or task-system level. CCTS remains narrower in its ontological claims and keeps structural coordination separate from claims about one unified cognitive subject.

## 4. Repository-specific contribution

CCTS contributes seven explicit requirements that are central to the repository's research method:

1. source-role provenance;
2. substantive reciprocal revision rather than coexistence;
3. claim-ceiling preservation;
4. authority separation;
5. rejected-branch preservation;
6. longitudinal re-entry through persistent artifacts;
7. implementation feedback without scientific over-promotion.

These requirements are repository-defined and are not claimed to be universally required by the external literature.

## 5. Executable structural contract

The repository implements CCTS as a typed, fail-closed structural audit. The implementation checks, among other conditions:

- required Human and AI contribution roles;
- an explicit problem-representation binding;
- substantive Human-to-AI and AI-to-Human revision or challenge paths;
- provenance and claim-boundary bindings;
- rejected-branch preservation;
- grounding declaration requirements;
- longitudinal artifact and re-entry bindings for the stronger profile;
- referential integrity for declared repository-artifact digests;
- declaration-level privacy and ontology boundaries.

The implementation is a structural QA mechanism.

```text
STRUCTURAL_CONFORMANCE != EMPIRICAL_MECHANISM
TEST_PASS != SCIENTIFIC_VALIDATION
VALID_DIGEST != SEMANTIC_TRUTH
```

## 6. Additive research surfaces

Recent CCTS-compatible work adds two narrow structural surfaces without changing core CCTS admission.

### 6.1 Relational-continuity claim review

A claim-local review surface distinguishes:
- `NOT_ASSERTED`;
- `EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`;
- `SUPPORTED_CANDIDATE`;
- `WEAKENED`;
- `UNRESOLVED`.

It rejects style similarity, memory availability, same-role labels and data continuity alone as sufficient evidence for relational continuity. Matched-information null results are treated as weakening evidence rather than test failures.

### 6.2 Human epistemic-agency retention design audit

A synthetic structural audit separates:
- AI-withheld baseline;
- CCTS / AI-available interaction;
- same-task AI-withheld judgement;
- content-distinct held-out AI-withheld judgement.

Its purpose is to detect design leakage and preserve the distinction between assisted performance and independent Human performance.

```text
AI_ASSISTED_OUTPUT_GAIN != INDEPENDENT_HUMAN_GAIN
STRUCTURAL_RETENTION_AUDIT_PASS != HUMAN_LEARNING
```

## 7. Falsification and weakening conditions

Future empirical claims about CCTS should be weakened if, after adequate controls:

- one-way high-quality assistance performs equivalently to reciprocal revision;
- reciprocal correction adds process cost without improving error detection, calibration or transfer;
- repository persistence produces only lexical reuse rather than structural continuity;
- fresh context reconstructs equivalent working state at comparable cost;
- Human independent performance degrades while joint performance improves;
- source-role provenance fails to improve attribution accuracy;
- implementation feedback increases confidence without improving correctness;
- apparent longitudinal effects are explained by model upgrades, retrieval, repeated exposure, Human practice or other confounds.

## 8. Scientific boundaries

CCTS is relevant to artificial-subjectivity research as a methodological surface, not as a positive subjectivity result.


## 9. Limitations

The present framework has important limitations.

First, the exact CCTS taxonomy is repository-defined and has not been independently validated as a distinct scientific construct.

Second, executable tests establish internal structural conformance only. They do not establish psychological mechanisms, Human learning, AI understanding, relational experience, identity continuity or causal effects.

Third, the current structural fixtures intentionally exclude private transcripts, Human identity and direct Human-participant observations. This protects privacy but means the present release is methodological rather than an empirical Human-subject study.

Fourth, the literature crosswalk establishes neighboring concepts, not novelty by subtraction. A stronger novelty claim requires continued systematic literature review and independent scholarly critique.

Fifth, a dedicated empirical program would require preregistered outcomes, negative controls, matched comparators, independent replication and venue-appropriate ethics review where real Human data are collected.
