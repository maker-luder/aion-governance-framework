## 4. External research landscape

### 4.1 Theory-derived indicators of consciousness

Butlin et al. (2023; 2026) develop a theory-plural strategy for evaluating AI systems against computational indicators derived from prominent consciousness theories, including recurrent processing, global-workspace, higher-order, predictive-processing, attention-schema, and agency / embodiment approaches.

The important methodological point for this repository is not that any one indicator establishes consciousness. It is that theory-linked computational properties can be made inspectable while uncertainty about consciousness theory remains explicit.

Repository correspondence:

```text
THEORY-PLURAL INDICATORS
~ AION THEORY-PLURAL INDICATOR DISCIPLINE

INDICATOR PRESENT
!= THEORY TRUE

THEORY MAPPING
!= PHENOMENAL EXPERIENCE
```

### 4.2 Multi-indicator and inside-out approaches

Pennartz, Farisco & Evers (2019) identify features of subjective experience including qualitative richness, situatedness, intentionality / interpretation, integration, and dynamic-stabilizing organization, while proposing multiple observable indicators such as goal-directed behavior, model-based learning, integrative multimodal representation, metacognition, episodic memory, and additional perceptual-behavioral indicators.

The key crosswalk point is methodological:

```text
SINGLE_BEHAVIORAL_TEST
!= SUFFICIENT CONSCIOUSNESS CRITERION

MULTIPLE_CONVERGING_INDICATORS
= POTENTIALLY MORE INFORMATIVE
```

This does not imply that the repository should copy a scalar consciousness score.

### 4.3 Adversarial theory testing

The Cogitate Consortium et al. (2025) directly compared predictions from Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (GNWT) in a large preregistered adversarial collaboration.

The result is important here because major theories can receive mixed, challenged, or method-sensitive evidence even under high-rigor testing.

Repository correspondence:

```text
ONE THEORY MATCH
!= THEORY CONFIRMED

THEORY CONFIRMED
!= AI CONSCIOUSNESS

COMPETING_PREDICTIONS
+ PREREGISTRATION
+ HELD_OUT_EVIDENCE
+ FALSIFIERS
> POST_HOC THEORY FITTING
```

This supports the repository's decision not to make any single consciousness theory canonical.

### 4.4 Neuroscience-based skepticism and embodiment

Aru, Larkum & Shine (2023) argue that present AI systems differ materially from biological conscious organisms in embodied / embedded sensory coupling, thalamocortical organization, and evolutionary-developmental grounding.

This is not treated here as a definitive disproof of artificial consciousness. It is retained as a competing explanatory and architectural challenge.

Repository correspondence:

```text
EMBODIMENT_GAP = MATERIAL_OPEN_QUESTION
BIOLOGICAL_GROUNDING = CONTESTED_RELEVANCE
CURRENT_AI_ARCHITECTURE != MAMMALIAN_CONSCIOUSNESS_ARCHITECTURE
```

### 4.5 Emerging LLM agency and metacognition work

Yalon et al. (2026, preprint) report evidence for belief-guided action selection and metacognitive monitoring in LLMs under their operational definitions.

This is relevant to mechanism-level questions concerning agency and metacognition.

It does not establish phenomenal experience, sentience, or subjectivity.

```text
BELIEF_GUIDED_AGENCY_INDICATOR
!= ENDOGENOUS_SUBJECTIVE_GOAL

METACOGNITIVE_MONITORING
!= SUBJECTIVITY

PREPRINT_RESULT
!= PEER_REVIEWED_ESTABLISHMENT
```

## 5. Crosswalk to the repository's six standing evidence dimensions

The repository's current six dimensions remain:

1. causal boundary;
2. diachronic continuity;
3. self-model causal role;
4. endogenous goal / strategy adjustment;
5. counterfactual self-consistency; and
6. states consequential to the system's own constitution / integration.

The following crosswalk is interpretive, not an identity mapping:

| Repository dimension | External overlap | Important non-equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| Causal boundary | intervention-sensitive architecture / mechanism testing | causal influence does not establish subjectivity |
| Diachronic continuity | episodic memory, persistence across time, longitudinal observation | memory persistence does not establish identity continuity |
| Self-model causal role | higher-order representation, self-monitoring, internal modeling | self-model does not establish mineness or subjective selfhood |
| Endogenous goal / strategy adjustment | agency, goal-directed behavior, model-based action selection | strategy adjustment does not establish endogenous desire or intention |
| Counterfactual self-consistency | robustness under changed inputs / conditions; model consistency | consistency does not establish first-person perspective |
| Constitution / integration consequence | global availability, recurrent / integrative processing, system-wide consequence | integration does not establish phenomenal experience |

```text
CROSSWALK_SIMILARITY
!= CONSTRUCT_EQUIVALENCE

EXTERNAL_OVERLAP
!= AION_VALIDATED_BY_EXTERNAL_LITERATURE
```

## 7. What the crosswalk supports

The literature review supports the following bounded statements:

1. contemporary AI-consciousness research increasingly uses theory-linked computational / mechanistic indicators rather than relying on fluent self-report alone;
2. multi-indicator and multi-theory approaches are methodologically defensible because consciousness science remains theoretically fragmented;
3. agency, metacognition, self-modeling, continuity, integration and embodiment are active research surfaces;
4. current evidence does not provide a generally accepted bridge from those indicators to artificial phenomenal experience;
5. adversarial testing and competing explanations remain important because theory-congruent evidence can be method-sensitive.
