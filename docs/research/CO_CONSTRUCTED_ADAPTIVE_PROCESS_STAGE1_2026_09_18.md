## 1. Terminology decision after external cross-check

`CO_CONSTRUCTED_ADAPTIVE_PROCESS` is retained as a **repository-local research hypothesis label**.

The phrase was originally proposed by ChatGPT Teacher as a working description of a process in which human meta-guidance, AI-local procedural selection, environmental feedback, and governance constraints jointly shape a recovery trajectory.

The Human Owner subsequently asked that the working term be promoted into an explicit research hypothesis, subject to literature cross-check and scientific boundaries.

A targeted literature search did **not** identify the exact phrase `co-constructed adaptive process` as an established standardized Human–AI construct. The search did identify several adjacent literatures:

- human–AI co-regulation and socially shared regulation;
- hybrid co-regulation in Human–AI collaboration;
- human–AI co-construction and preference-based cooperation;
- mixed-initiative adaptive agents and dynamically allocated agency; and
- adaptation in collaborative regulation after obstacles or breakdowns.

Therefore:

```text
CO_CONSTRUCTED_ADAPTIVE_PROCESS
= REPOSITORY_LOCAL_HYPOTHESIS_LABEL

CO_CONSTRUCTED_ADAPTIVE_PROCESS
!= ESTABLISHED_EXTERNAL_CONSTRUCT

LITERATURE_ADJACENCY
!= CONSTRUCT_VALIDATION
```

The term may remain only while it adds discriminant value beyond existing `CO_REGULATION`, `CCTS`, `BIDIRECTIONAL_GROUNDING`, `MIXED_INITIATIVE`, and ordinary externally instructed strategy change.

## 4. Candidate hypothesis H-CCAP-1

### 4.1 Hypothesis statement

```text
H-CCAP-1:

UNDER A FIXED EXTERNALLY SUPPLIED GOAL,
IF A HUMAN PROVIDES HIGH-LEVEL META-GUIDANCE THAT PERMITS ADAPTATION
WITHOUT FULLY SPECIFYING THE LOCAL RECOVERY PROCEDURE,
AND THE ENVIRONMENT RETURNS ACTIONABLE OBSTACLE FEEDBACK,
THEN A HUMAN-AI-SYSTEM INTERACTION MAY PRODUCE
A GOAL-PRESERVING, CONSTRAINT-PRESERVING PROCEDURAL REVISION
WHOSE CONCRETE RECOVERY TRAJECTORY IS JOINTLY CONDITIONED BY:

- HUMAN META-GUIDANCE,
- AI/SYSTEM LOCAL PROCEDURE SELECTION,
- ENVIRONMENTAL FEEDBACK,
- TOOL AFFORDANCES,
- GOVERNANCE CONSTRAINTS,
- AND AVAILABLE INTERACTION HISTORY.
```

The proposed name for this interaction-level candidate is:

```text
CO_CONSTRUCTED_ADAPTIVE_PROCESS
```

### 4.2 What `co-constructed` means here

`Co-constructed` does **not** mean equal control, symmetric cognition, shared consciousness, shared intention, or shared agency.

It means only that the observed trajectory is not adequately described by looking at one input source alone.

```text
CO_CONSTRUCTED
= MULTI-SOURCE_INTERACTIONAL_CONDITIONING

CO_CONSTRUCTED
!= SYMMETRIC_COGNITION
!= SHARED_MIND
!= SHARED_PHENOMENAL_STATE
!= EQUAL_CAUSAL_CONTRIBUTION
```

### 4.3 What `adaptive` means here

`Adaptive` is used behaviorally and procedurally:

```text
OBSTACLE_FEEDBACK
+ PROCEDURE_REVISION
+ GOAL_PRESERVATION
+ CONSTRAINT_PRESERVATION
```

It does not by itself establish learning or endogenous agency.

```text
ADAPTIVE_BEHAVIOR
!= LEARNING
!= MODEL_SELF_MODIFICATION
!= ENDOGENOUS_MOTIVATION
```

## 9. Falsifiers / reduction conditions

H-CCAP-1 should be narrowed, absorbed into an existing construct, or rejected if controlled work shows any of the following:

1. the concrete recovery procedure is fully specified by the human instruction;
2. all observed strategy changes are deterministic consequences of a single tool or repository rule and require no additional interaction-level representation;
3. `co-regulation`, `mixed-initiative`, `bidirectional grounding`, or ordinary error recovery predicts the same outcomes without loss;
4. removing human meta-guidance does not change any relevant recovery behavior under matched conditions;
5. removing environmental feedback does not change any relevant recovery behavior;
6. the proposed factorization cannot be scored reproducibly by independent operators;
7. apparent adaptation is only repeated trial-and-error without goal or constraint preservation;
8. the construct encourages inference of shared agency, shared cognition, or subjectivity from ordinary coordination.

### 11.2 Hybrid co-regulation

Zhang et al. (2026), *Human-AI Collaboration Reconfigures Group Regulation from Socially Shared to Hybrid Co-Regulation*, report a parallel-group randomized study in which GenAI availability changed distributions of collaborative regulation among 71 university students.

Source: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08344`

This supports the general proposition that Human–AI participation can alter regulation patterns in a collaborative setting. It does not establish the present repository-local process or AI subjectivity.

### 11.3 Human–AI co-construction and adaptive policy

Dutta et al. (2025), *Problem Solving Through Human–AI Preference-based Cooperation*, propose HAI-Co², a human–AI co-construction framework in which complex solutions and objectives can be iteratively revised, and agent behavior can depend on informational state and policy.

DOI: `10.1162/coli.a.19`

ACL Anthology: `https://aclanthology.org/2025.cl-4.8/`

This is especially close terminology-wise, but remains distinct:

```text
HAI_CO2_CO_CONSTRUCTION
!= CO_CONSTRUCTED_ADAPTIVE_PROCESS
```

The current hypothesis focuses specifically on obstacle-triggered procedural revision under distributed human, AI/system, environment, tool and governance constraints.

### 11.4 Mixed-initiative adaptive agents

Natarajan (2025), *Adaptive Agents for Mixed-Initiative Human-AI Collaborations*, studies adaptive agents in mixed-initiative settings where humans and agents dynamically contribute to decisions and actions.

DOI: `10.1609/aaai.v39i28.35220`

This is a candidate comparison for dynamic contribution and agency allocation, not evidence that the current event instantiated shared or autonomous agency.
