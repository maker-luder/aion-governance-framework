## Scope and provenance

This is the bounded disposition of `CCTS-LIT-FRESHNESS-01` in the [cross-layer audit](CROSS_LAYER_RESEARCH_ENGINEERING_GAP_AUDIT_2026_09_25.md). **CCTS here means the repository-defined Co-Constructed Thinking Space / 共構思考場域, a local Human Owner working concept**, not a term or scale imported from external literature. The [CCTS preprint draft](publication/CCTS_PREPRINT_DRAFT_V0_1.md) is the definition source: CCTS is a bounded Human–AI interaction-and-artifact structure with an explicit problem representation, substantive reciprocal revision or challenge, source-role provenance, claim boundaries, Human authority separation and rejected-branch preservation. Its stronger longitudinal repository profile additionally binds external evidence, artifacts, implementation feedback, persistence and re-entry. This document supplements, but does not retroactively alter, the already published preprint or its archived PDF.

AE-AI is an **external study of student agency in AI-assisted learning**. It neither defines CCTS nor measures whether a local interaction satisfies the CCTS structural contract. The comparison below asks where its factors are conceptually adjacent and where CCTS's own requirements remain unmeasured. The preprint already rejects a global-novelty claim; this crosswalk does not assert an exact external equivalent.

Primary bibliographic locator: Dai, Y., Liu, S., Zhou, S., Lai, S., Liu, A., & Lim, C. P. (2026), “Redefining and measuring student agency in AI-assisted learning: Development and validation of the agentic engagement with AI (AE-AI) scale,” *Computers & Education* 253, 105687, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2026.105687. The [institutional publication record](https://research.cuhk.edu.hk/en/publications/redefining-and-measuring-student-agency-in-ai-assisted-learning-d/) reports 26 interviews, an initial 28-item pool, exploratory factor analysis with 340 participants, confirmatory factor analysis with 256 participants, and a final 16-item, four-factor scale. This is evidence about the reported AE-AI study, not independent replication of it or validation of CCTS.

| External AE-AI factor | Conceptual adjacency to the local 共構思考場域 | CCTS requirement not established by that factor |
|---|---|---|
| Adaptive Direction | Human redirection may occur within a shared inquiry. | Redirection alone does not show that Human and AI materially revise or challenge one another in both directions, or that an explicit problem representation is maintained. |
| Critical Integration | Critical assessment may contribute to an epistemic revision. | The factor does not supply CCTS source-role, claim-boundary, authority, or rejected-branch bindings. A CCTS structural audit must inspect the interaction and artifacts independently. |
| Cross-Source Inquiry | External-source use may mediate longitudinal CCTS work. | Cross-source behavior alone does not establish the stronger repository profile's exact artifact/digest binding, implementation evidence or re-entry continuity. |
| Reflective Calibration | Human reflection may be present during reciprocal inquiry. | Reflection neither proves the CCTS core structural contract nor demonstrates delayed AI-withheld Human transfer or a causal learning benefit; those are separate questions. |

Another adjacent measure predates this paper: Xia, L., Shen, K., Sun, H., An, X., & Dong, Y. (2025), “Developing and validating the student learning agency scale in generative artificial intelligence (AI)-supported contexts,” *Education and Information Technologies* 30, 13999–14021, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-024-13137-5. Its [publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10639-024-13137-5) describes a different student-agency scale. These two examples are not a systematic review.
