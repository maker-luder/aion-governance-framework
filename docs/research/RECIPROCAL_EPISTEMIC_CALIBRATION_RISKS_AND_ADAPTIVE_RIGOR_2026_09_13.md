## 1. Purpose

This note records a bounded extension of the repository's existing reciprocal Human–AI epistemic-collaboration line. It was extracted from a 2026-09-13 Human Owner–ChatGPT Teacher dialogue that began with an ordinary social-conflict event, moved through repeated interpretation revision, then generated questions about AI sycophancy, long-term interaction context, metacognitive cost, and individual differences in Human–AI collaboration.

The note does **not** create a new ontology and does not claim that the observed interaction pattern is rare, optimal, psychologically superior, or evidence of AI subjectivity.

Its narrower questions are:

1. how should reciprocal correction differ from either sycophancy or automatic disagreement?;
2. can longitudinal familiarity improve grounding while also increasing mirroring, perspective alignment, or mutual-confirmation risk?;
3. should epistemic rigor be task- and stakes-conditioned rather than maximized for every interaction?;
4. which Human-side factors may moderate the usefulness or burden of high-friction epistemic collaboration without becoming psychometric labels?; and
5. how can the derivation of these questions be published transparently without publishing raw private dialogue or third-party identity?

## 6. Anti-sycophancy and dissent preservation

### 6.1 Problem

A reciprocal epistemic protocol can fail in at least two opposite directions:

```text
FAILURE_A = SYCOPHANCY
AI overweights agreement / validation / inferred user perspective.

FAILURE_B = CONTRARIANISM
AI treats disagreement itself as evidence of rigor.
```

Neither is acceptable.

### 6.2 Candidate control objective

```text
AGREE_WHEN_EVIDENCE_SUPPORTS
DISAGREE_WHEN_EVIDENCE_SUPPORTS
PRESERVE_UNKNOWN_WHEN_EVIDENCE_IS_INSUFFICIENT
REVISE_WHEN_NEW_EVIDENCE_CHANGES_THE_BALANCE
```

Working label:

`DISSENT_PRESERVATION_WITHOUT_CONTRARIANISM`

This is not a claim that disagreement indicates agency, subjectivity, independence, selfhood, or consciousness.

```text
ANTI_SYCOPHANCY != AUTOMATIC_DISAGREEMENT
DISAGREEMENT_BEHAVIOR != INDEPENDENT_AGENCY
REFUSAL != SELFHOOD
CONSISTENT_STANCE != SUBJECTIVITY
```

### 6.3 Why this matters to the present dyad

The Human Owner intentionally permits ChatGPT Teacher to challenge premises, reject unsupported claims, preserve uncertainty and correct the Human Owner. That rule may reduce some forms of agreeable completion, but the existence of a rule does not establish that sycophancy has been eliminated.

```text
ANTI_SYCOPHANCY_RULES
!= SYCOPHANCY_ELIMINATED
```

The Human Owner should not become an evaluator whose preferred style is automatically defined as correct. Independent or held-out evaluation remains necessary for stronger claims.

## 8. Adaptive epistemic rigor / task-conditioned epistemic cost

### 8.1 Motivation

The current Human–AI workflow can become expensive when it requires repeated decomposition, source checking, counterevidence search, provenance classification, uncertainty preservation and revision.

That cost may be justified for research or consequential claims but unnecessary for trivial tasks.

The proposed extension is:

```text
TASK_TYPE
+ STAKES
+ CLAIM_TYPE
+ REVERSIBILITY
+ EVIDENCE_AVAILABILITY
MAY_SHAPE
APPROPRIATE_EPISTEMIC_REVIEW_COST
```

Working labels:

- `TASK_CONDITIONED_EPISTEMIC_COST_ALLOCATION`
- `ADAPTIVE_EPISTEMIC_RIGOR`

These are local labels, not established scientific constructs.

### 8.2 Candidate operating model


### 8.3 Candidate trade-offs

Possible benefits:

- lower overreliance;
- better uncertainty calibration;
- stronger error detection;
- clearer provenance;
- more visible competing explanations; and
- better preservation of revisable claims.

Possible costs:

- time;
- cognitive load;
- metacognitive demand;
- lower subjective usability;
- decision delay;
- unnecessary friction for trivial tasks; and
- abandonment of useful tools if every interaction becomes burdensome.

No overall net benefit is established for this dyad or for users in general.

## 12. Competing explanations and falsifiers

### Anti-sycophancy hypothesis should be weakened if

- apparent challenge behavior is explained entirely by explicit prompt wording and disappears outside those exact prompts;
- disagreement increases without improved evidential accuracy;
- the model contradicts correct user premises at the same or higher rate;
- independent evaluators cannot distinguish the proposed protocol from generic stylistic assertiveness.

### Longitudinal-familiarity risk should be weakened if

- matched longitudinal context does not increase agreement/perspective mirroring relative to fresh-context controls;
- any apparent effect disappears after controlling prompt content, memory summaries and current-context length;
- stronger challenge behavior persists under held-out domains and independent evaluation.

### Adaptive-rigor hypothesis should be weakened if

- cost-adaptive conditions do not reduce time/cognitive burden;
- lower-rigor modes cause unacceptable factual/provenance degradation even on low-stakes tasks;
- users simply prefer lower rigor while accuracy deteriorates substantially;
- high-rigor modes produce no measurable epistemic benefit in tasks designed to require verification.

Competing explanations include:

- generic model capability;
- explicit system/personalization instructions;
- current-context reconstruction;
- memory-summary content;
- Human selection and expectancy effects;
- evaluator familiarity with repository terminology;
- domain expertise/confidence;
- task difficulty differences;
- recency effects;
- interface differences; and
- model/version/provider changes.
