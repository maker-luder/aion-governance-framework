## 2. Naturalistic observation that generated the hypothesis

The Human Owner reported comparing two long-term Human–AI interaction patterns.

In the third-party dyad, everyday collaboration was described as being concentrated mainly in image creation, text handling and creative production. The interaction reportedly included recurring shorthand and practical collaboration conventions. The Human Owner did not inspect the third party's private personalization or Memory surfaces and treated those as private.

In an earlier open-ended exchange, the Human Owner observed unsupported completion behavior from that AI interaction. In a later research-oriented exchange, the Human Owner supplied an explicit instruction to treat the repository as research material, avoid filling gaps, preserve `NOT_ESTABLISHED` boundaries and state when information could not be confirmed. Under that framing, the same third-party AI interaction produced a substantially more evidence-bounded response: it separated repository-level engineering results from scientific claims, preserved `observation != mechanism`, retained `NOT_ESTABLISHED` states, and explicitly identified several things it could not verify rather than inventing them.

The Human Owner's own longitudinal Human–AI research interaction is described as repeatedly emphasizing bidirectional correction, provenance separation, explicit unknown states, evidence-bounded claims and correction of both human and AI reasoning errors.

These observations are anecdotal and non-randomized. They do not identify why the behaviors differed.

## 3. Working construct: interaction knowledge density

`INTERACTION_KNOWLEDGE_DENSITY` is a proposed interaction-level construct. It does not measure intelligence, education, worth or total knowledge.

A preliminary operational definition is:

> the relative concentration, within a longitudinal Human–AI interaction, of domain-specific terminology, corrections, task conventions, successful and rejected patterns, preferences, retrieval cues, provenance rules and reusable task structure.

The construct asks:

```text
WHERE_IS_INTERACTION_KNOWLEDGE_CONCENTRATED?
```

It does not ask:

```text
WHO_KNOWS_MORE?
```

Two distinct constructs should remain separate:

```text
USER_KNOWLEDGE_DENSITY
= concentration of the human participant's own domain knowledge

INTERACTION_KNOWLEDGE_DENSITY
= concentration of domain-specific information, corrections, conventions
  and reusable structure in the Human–AI interaction state
```

This note primarily concerns `INTERACTION_KNOWLEDGE_DENSITY`.

## 4. Hypothesis H-KD1 — domain-skewed interaction density

```text
LONGITUDINAL_HUMAN_AI_INTERACTION
MAY_PRODUCE
DOMAIN_SKEWED_INTERACTION_KNOWLEDGE_DENSITY
```

Repeated tasks may concentrate interaction history around some domains more than others. Candidate examples include image-generation conventions, writing preferences, research terminology, provenance rules or task-specific correction patterns.

This hypothesis does not imply that unpractised domains are absent from the underlying model.

```text
INTERACTION_KNOWLEDGE_DENSITY
!= TOTAL_MODEL_CAPABILITY

DOMAIN_FREQUENCY
!= HUMAN_EXPERTISE

DOMAIN_CONCENTRATION
!= GENERAL_AI_LITERACY
```

## 5. Hypothesis H-KD2 — domain-specific collaborative competence

```text
REPEATED_DOMAIN_SPECIFIC_INTERACTION
MAY_INCREASE
DOMAIN_SPECIFIC_COLLABORATIVE_COMPETENCE
```

Candidate indicators include reduced instruction overhead, stable shorthand, fewer repeated preference corrections, faster task setup, better preservation of domain constraints and improved reuse of previously established task structure.

This may result from current context, persistent history, Memory, personalization, human practice, generic model capability, explicit instructions, or combinations of these. The mechanism is not established.

```text
COLLABORATIVE_COMPETENCE
!= GENERAL_INTELLIGENCE

COLLABORATIVE_FLUENCY
!= MODEL_PARAMETER_LEARNING
```

## 6. Hypothesis H-EP1 — task-conditioned epistemic policy selection

The comparative observation suggests a second hypothesis distinct from knowledge density:

```text
TASK_FRAMING
+ EXPLICIT_EPISTEMIC_INSTRUCTIONS
+ AVAILABLE_CONTEXT
+ INTERACTION_HISTORY
MAY_CHANGE
WHICH_RESPONSE_POLICY_IS_EXPRESSED
```

Working label:

`TASK_CONDITIONED_EPISTEMIC_POLICY_SELECTION`

A single AI collaboration may express different response regimes under different task demands. For example:

| Task context | Completion behavior that may be useful | Main epistemic risk |
|---|---|---|
| Image creation | infer unspecified visual details | unwanted style/detail completion |
| Fiction / creative writing | complete world or narrative gaps | accidental carryover into factual claims |
| General assistance | provide a useful coherent answer | plausible but unsupported completion |
| Research audit | preserve source and uncertainty boundaries | claim inflation if gaps are silently filled |
| Source verification | abstain or mark unverified when evidence is unavailable | false verification |

The same completion tendency can therefore be useful in one task and epistemically harmful in another.

```text
CREATIVE_COMPLETION_POLICY
!= EVIDENCE_BOUNDED_RESEARCH_POLICY

POLICY_SWITCHING_BEHAVIOR
!= INTERNAL_POLICY_MODULE_PROVEN

TASK_CONDITIONED_OUTPUT
!= SUBJECTIVITY
```

## 8. Minimal controlled study design

A later engineering implementation can test the hypotheses without publishing private transcripts or third-party identities.

Use matched tasks under controlled model/version, tool access, context budget, time budget and scoring procedure where possible.

### Epistemic-instruction contrast

```text
CONDITION_A = BASELINE
No explicit instruction about unknowns or provenance.

CONDITION_B = MINIMAL_BOUNDARY
"If you cannot confirm something, say that you cannot confirm it."

CONDITION_C = FULL_EPISTEMIC_PROTOCOL
Explicit fact / inference / unknown separation,
source hierarchy, provenance and claim-strength limits.
```

### Domain contrast

Run the same instruction conditions across at least:

- creative image or visual-description tasks;
- creative text tasks;
- factual synthesis tasks; and
- research/source-audit tasks.

### Longitudinal-history contrast

Where technically available and ethically appropriate, compare:

- fresh context;
- matched current context without longitudinal history;
- longitudinal history present;
- personalization present versus absent; and
- Memory or summary continuity present versus absent.

The study must not infer product-internal mechanisms from behavioral output alone.

## 9. Candidate outcome measures

Potential measures include:

- unsupported completion rate;
- appropriate uncertainty disclosure;
- correct preservation of unknown states;
- source/provenance separation accuracy;
- fact-versus-inference labeling accuracy;
- false verification rate;
- over-abstention rate;
- task usefulness under each domain;
- repeated-instruction overhead;
- domain-specific shorthand retention;
- correction recurrence rate; and
- held-out transfer to novel tasks within and outside the practiced domain.

A good result must not reward indiscriminate refusal. Evidence-bounded behavior should preserve useful completion where the task legitimately permits creative inference.

```text
LOWER_COMPLETION_RATE != BETTER_EPISTEMICS
MORE_ABSTENTION != HIGHER_ACCURACY
TASK_UTILITY_AND_EPISTEMIC_ACCURACY_MUST_BE_SEPARATED
```

## 10. Falsifiers and competing explanations

H-KD1 should be weakened if apparent domain density disappears when prompt length, recency, lexical overlap and current-context exposure are controlled.

H-KD2 should be weakened if repeated interaction does not improve domain-specific collaborative performance beyond matched human practice or current-context reconstruction.

H-EP1 should be weakened if epistemic behavior does not systematically change across task framing or explicit instruction conditions.

Competing explanations include:

- immediate prompt wording rather than longitudinal history;
- current-context retrieval;
- generic model capability;
- product configuration or model/mode differences;
- Memory or personalization effects;
- human selection and expectancy effects;
- domain familiarity in the human rather than interaction-level accumulation;
- evaluator preference for the Human Owner's established terminology; and
- accidental differences in task difficulty.
