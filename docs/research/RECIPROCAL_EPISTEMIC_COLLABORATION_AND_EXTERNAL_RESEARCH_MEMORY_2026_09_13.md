## 1. Purpose

This note records a Human Owner observation and a bounded research formalization about two connected but non-identical questions:

1. how a long-running Human–AI collaboration can treat correction as reciprocal rather than one-directional; and
2. whether a versioned public repository can function as an external epistemic continuity scaffold so that research rules, provenance, hypotheses, failures and unresolved questions remain inspectable after context loss, account change, collaborator change, or ordinary human forgetting.

The Human Owner's current metaphor is that the repository has become a **large research library**: if a detail is forgotten later, or a new external Human/AI collaborator re-enters the project, the surviving artifacts should make the earlier work recoverable and challengeable.

This note does **not** claim that a Git repository is biological memory, that an AI has autobiographical memory, that repository persistence proves identity continuity, or that reciprocal correction is rare among users.

## 4. External research crosswalk

### 4.1 Coactive design and directability

Coactive-design literature treats human–automation teamwork as interdependent rather than purely command-and-execute. Its OPD framework includes `directability`, described in later human-factors literature as the ability of human and technical agents to influence each other's behavior.

Relevant source:
- Adriaensen et al. (2022), *Teaming with industrial cobots: A socio-technical perspective on safety analysis*, DOI `10.1002/hfm.20939`.

### 4.3 Cognitive offloading

Risko and Gilbert define cognitive offloading as using external action or structure to alter the information-processing demands of a task; reminders and other external aids are canonical examples. The review also emphasizes that offloading decisions depend partly on metacognitive judgments and can be suboptimal.

Source:
- Risko & Gilbert (2016), *Cognitive Offloading*, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 20(9):676-688, DOI `10.1016/j.tics.2016.07.002`.

The repository analogy is bounded:

```text
REPOSITORY_CAN_REDUCE_INTERNAL_RECALL_DEMAND = PLAUSIBLE_ANALOGY
REPOSITORY_USE == HUMAN_MEMORY = FALSE
MORE_OFFLOADING == BETTER_LEARNING = NOT_ESTABLISHED
```

Externalizing research may improve recoverability while also creating dependence if the human can no longer reconstruct, critique or transfer the method without retrieval.

### 4.4 FAIR research-object stewardship

The FAIR principles define Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability and Reusability for digital research objects and explicitly include detailed provenance and workflows as relevant to transparent, reusable research infrastructure.

Source:
- Wilkinson et al. (2016), *The FAIR Guiding Principles for scientific data management and stewardship*, Scientific Data 3:160018, DOI `10.1038/sdata.2016.18`.

## 5. Candidate externalized collaboration protocol

The present collaboration can be described, without claiming novelty, by the following candidate protocol:

```text
RECIPROCAL_CORRECTION
Human may challenge AI.
AI may challenge Human.

EVIDENCE_OVERRIDES_STATUS
Neither participant receives automatic epistemic priority merely by role.

UNKNOWN_PRESERVATION
Insufficient evidence remains UNKNOWN rather than being filled for fluency.

PROVENANCE_SEPARATION
Human-origin, AI-formalized, joint-synthesis and external-source contributions remain distinguishable.

CLAIM_CEILING
The conclusion cannot exceed the evidential layer that supports it.

COUNTEREVIDENCE_RETENTION
Disconfirming material is retained rather than silently edited away.

RECOVERABILITY
A later participant should be able to reconstruct the live research question, boundaries and unresolved alternatives from artifacts.

CONTESTABILITY_AFTER_REENTRY
A later participant may challenge inherited repository claims rather than treating persistence as truth.
```

The repository is valuable under this model only if it makes these rules inspectable and reusable; the Git history itself is not an epistemic authority.

## 6. External epistemic memory scaffold hypothesis

### Human-side observation

A human participant may lose immediate recall while external research artifacts remain available. A later return can therefore begin from surviving records rather than from the original private conversational state.

### Teacher formalization

Candidate hypothesis:

> A versioned repository containing explicit provenance, uncertainty, correction history, claim ceilings and navigation may reduce the cost of epistemic re-entry after context discontinuity, compared with a condition in which only unstructured or decontextualized outputs survive.

This is deliberately weaker than claiming recovered identity, recovered relationship state, or recovered subjective memory.

Candidate measurable outputs include:

- time or steps required to locate the active research question;
- proportion of provenance roles reconstructed correctly;
- preservation of explicit `UNKNOWN` states;
- number of stale/superseded claims incorrectly treated as current;
- ability to identify unresolved competing explanations;
- ability to distinguish repository content from current external evidence;
- ability to continue the problem with a different collaborator without inventing missing history.

## 7. Competing explanations and risks

Any later study must preserve at least these alternatives:

1. **Search convenience only** — lower re-entry cost may be ordinary document search, not a distinctive epistemic-memory effect.
2. **Prompt reconstruction** — a new AI may reproduce the old style because files explicitly instruct it to do so.
3. **Human carryover** — the returning Human may supply the missing norms from personal memory rather than the repository.
4. **Selection bias** — only successful or valued interaction rules may have been documented.
5. **Documentation illusion** — a rule may exist in text without being retrieved or causally activated.
6. **Cognitive dependence** — better retrieval may coexist with reduced independent reconstruction or transfer.
7. **Stale-memory hazard** — preserved text may be outdated and incorrectly treated as current.
8. **Authority leakage** — historical AI wording may be mistaken for Human Owner approval or current governance.

```text
REENTRY_SUCCESS != IDENTITY_CONTINUITY
REENTRY_SUCCESS != LEARNING
REENTRY_SUCCESS != SUBJECTIVITY
DOCUMENTED_RULE != CAUSALLY_ACTIVE_RULE
RETRIEVED_RECORD != CURRENT_TRUTH
```
