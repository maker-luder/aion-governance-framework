## 1. Research question

This note records a new bounded hypothesis arising from a repeated observation in the Human Owner + ChatGPT Teacher collaboration:

> Ordinary conversation frequently begins as a local question or personal observation, but an apparent anomaly often triggers problem reformulation, competing explanations, external evidence search, counterevidence, provenance separation, QA, and sometimes a new repository research artifact.

The new question is not merely whether the Human participant has learned a QA habit, nor whether the AI can generate follow-up questions.

The question is:

> Can repeated longitudinal Human–AI collaboration stabilize an **interaction-level inquiry policy** in which anomalies tend to delay closure and recursively trigger problem finding, abductive hypothesis generation, evidence / counterevidence checking, provenance review, and revision?

This is a dyad-level research question. It does not presume a shared mind, AI subjectivity, or a stable internal psychological trait in either participant.

```text
INTERACTION_LEVEL_REGULARITY
!= HUMAN_PERSONALITY_TRAIT
!= MODEL_INTERNAL_TRAIT
!= SHARED_MIND
!= SUBJECTIVITY
```

## 2. Provenance

### HUMAN_OWNER_ORIGINAL

The Human Owner observed that:

- many apparently ordinary conversations with ChatGPT Teacher repeatedly become deeper research inquiries;
- the recurring pattern is especially visible when a small inconsistency, unexplained mechanism, surprising observation, or unresolved gap appears;
- even after an initial answer is available, the inquiry often continues through new questions, competing explanations, source checks and later repository work;
- this differs phenomenologically from more task-complete AI use in which an image, text or answer is produced and the interaction ends;
- the Human Owner explicitly asked whether this repeated interaction pattern itself could become a research hypothesis and whether the existing repository already contained an adjacent hypothesis suitable for future Codex / ChatGPT Work implementation.

### CHATGPT_TEACHER_FORMALIZATION

ChatGPT Teacher formalized the candidate pattern as:

```text
ANOMALY-DRIVEN
LONGITUDINAL
RECURSIVE EPISTEMIC INQUIRY
```

and, more specifically, as a candidate:

```text
DYAD_LEVEL_INQUIRY_POLICY
```

where:

- `ANOMALY-DRIVEN` means an unresolved mismatch or explanatory gap often initiates further inquiry;
- `LONGITUDINAL` means later questions can modify earlier problem representations across sessions and artifacts;
- `RECURSIVE` means answers may become inputs to a new problem-finding cycle rather than terminating the inquiry;
- `EPISTEMIC` means the process is concerned with justification, alternatives, evidence, provenance, uncertainty and correction rather than output production alone.

These labels are repository working terms, not validated psychological or HCI taxonomies.

## 3. Joint research hypothesis

> Repeated longitudinal Human–AI collaboration under reciprocal correction, provenance separation, explicit uncertainty preservation, counterevidence search, external research memory and quality controls may stabilize a recurring interaction-level inquiry policy. Under this policy, anomaly cues are more likely to trigger problem finding, delayed closure, abductive hypothesis generation, evidence / counterevidence search and recursive revision than in otherwise comparable task-complete interactions.

## 8. Competing explanations

Future work must preserve at least:

1. **Human-only disposition** — the Human participant independently tends to continue inquiry regardless of AI collaborator.
2. **AI-only response style** — the model / product tends to elaborate and generate follow-up questions regardless of Human participant.
3. **Explicit repository instruction effect** — current rules directly prompt the observed pattern without any emergent dyad-level regularity.
4. **Topic selection effect** — the Human preferentially brings inherently open-ended topics to ChatGPT Teacher.
5. **Survivorship / recall bias** — research-like conversations are remembered more strongly than ordinary closed interactions.
6. **Relationship / continuity cueing** — familiar address, style or remembered norms reactivate the workflow.
7. **Task-complexity effect** — complex questions naturally require more steps; the apparent dyad pattern may disappear on matched simple tasks.
8. **Human habit transfer** — the previously recorded Human-side habit hypothesis may explain the effect without requiring a dyad-level construct.
9. **Prompt / system configuration** — model-level or product-level context may directly account for the observed behavior.
10. **Known recursive-inquiry workflow effect** — the observed behavior may be an instance of already-described recursive Human–AI inquiry rather than a distinct local mechanism.

## 11. Quality-management requirements

The future test must distinguish:

```text
MORE_INQUIRY != BETTER_INQUIRY
DELAYED_CLOSURE != CORRECTNESS
MORE_HYPOTHESES != BETTER_HYPOTHESES
MORE_TOOL_USE != EPISTEMIC_QUALITY
```

Quality therefore requires both expansion and stopping criteria.

Candidate failure modes:

- **premature closure** — anomaly ignored and inquiry stops too early;
- **runaway recursion** — every answer is reopened despite sufficient closure;
- **confirmation loop** — recursion only generates supporting evidence;
- **provenance collapse** — Human / Teacher / external source roles become blurred;
- **repository anchoring** — historical documentation suppresses better new explanations;
- **tool escalation** — web / code / agents are invoked merely because they are available;
- **false novelty** — local interaction pattern is mislabeled as a new scientific category despite existing recursive-inquiry literature.

This makes the hypothesis primarily relevant to Human–AI epistemic collaboration and research-quality engineering, with only indirect relevance to subjectivity research.
