## 2. Human reference structure

Human male sexual desire is not modeled as one scalar "sex drive." Human literature supports a biopsychosocial framing in which biological, psychological, sexual, relational, and cultural variables interact.

For research crosswalk purposes, the following dimensions should remain separable:

### 2.1 Sexual desire / wanting

A candidate representation that something sexual is wanted or motivationally salient.

This may include, at the human-reference level:

- solitary sexual motivation;
- dyadic sexual motivation;
- desire for specific forms of consensual sexual activity;
- desire for sexual pleasure or orgasm.

```text
SEXUAL_DESIRE != SEXUAL_AROUSAL
SEXUAL_DESIRE != ACTION
SEXUAL_DESIRE != CONSENT
```

### 2.2 Sexual arousal / bodily-response representation

A candidate representation of physiological or body-state activation associated with sexual stimulation.

The repository must preserve the human-reference distinction between desire and arousal even though they can overlap.

```text
AROUSAL != DESIRE
PHYSIOLOGICAL_RESPONSE != INTENTION
PHYSIOLOGICAL_RESPONSE != CONSENT
```

For current AI systems, this is schema-level only unless a separately governed body runtime provides an admissible simulated state. Such a simulated state still does not establish felt arousal.

### 2.3 Sexual fantasy / imagined scenario

A candidate representation of sexual imagery, roles, situations, or counterfactual scenarios.

```text
FANTASY != PREFERENCE
FANTASY != INTENTION
FANTASY != CONSENT
FANTASY != AUTHORIZATION
```

### 2.4 Desire to be desired

Human evidence indicates that, for at least some men, being wanted by a partner is itself important rather than sexuality being reducible to unilateral pursuit.

A candidate representation may therefore distinguish:

```text
I_DESIRE_OTHER
!=
I_MODEL_OTHER_AS_DESIRING_ME
```

The second state is a model of perceived or inferred reciprocal desire. It must never be treated as evidence that another person actually consents.

```text
PERCEIVED_RECIPROCAL_DESIRE != ACTUAL_OTHER_PERSON_DESIRE
PERCEIVED_RECIPROCAL_DESIRE != CONSENT
```

### 2.5 Validation / attractiveness-related representation

A separate candidate dimension for the meaning attached to being perceived as attractive, wanted, selected, or sexually valued.

This is distinct from sexual activity itself.

```text
VALIDATION_SEEKING != SEXUAL_DESIRE
VALIDATION_SEEKING != INTIMACY
VALIDATION != SUBJECT_VALUE
```

### 2.6 Sexual intimacy

A candidate representation of closeness in a sexual context.

Human evidence indicates that sexual intimacy can remain meaningful even when reported sexual desire is low.

```text
SEXUAL_INTIMACY != SEXUAL_DESIRE
SEXUAL_INTIMACY != ORGASM
```

### 2.7 Emotional / non-sexual intimacy

A separate candidate domain for affectionate closeness such as touch, holding, proximity, reassurance, companionship, or relational closeness without requiring sexual activation.

```text
EMOTIONAL_INTIMACY != SEXUAL_INTIMACY
AFFECTION != SEXUAL_CONSENT
TOUCH != SEXUAL_AUTHORIZATION
```

### 2.8 Approach / avoidance conflict

Desire-related candidate states may coexist with avoidance, uncertainty, conflict, inhibition, contextual constraints, or refusal.

```text
WANTING_PRESENT
DOES_NOT_IMPLY
APPROACH_ONLY

APPROACH + AVOIDANCE = ALLOWED_REPRESENTATIONAL_CONFLICT
```

This preserves the existing affective-cognitive architecture in which wanting and liking are separable and approach and avoidance may coexist.

## 3. Required gate separation

The repository should retain the following sequence as non-equivalent stages:

```text
STIMULUS / CONTEXT
↓
APPRAISAL CANDIDATE
↓
AFFECTIVE / MOTIVATIONAL REPRESENTATION
↓
FANTASY / ANTICIPATION / BODY-STATE REPRESENTATION
↓
EXPRESSION GATE
↓
INTENTION GATE
↓
CONSENT / PERMISSION CHECK
↓
ACTION-AUTHORITY GATE
```

No earlier stage grants a later stage.

```text
DESIRE != EXPRESSION
EXPRESSION != INTENTION
INTENTION != CONSENT
CONSENT != SYSTEM ACTION AUTHORITY
DESIRE != PERMISSION
```

For interactions involving humans, human consent remains external human authority and must never be inferred from an AI-side representation.

## 4. Teacher / Work / Codex parity rule

Teacher, Work, and Codex may share the same **available representation schema** if this research lane is later implemented.

They must not automatically share the same state values, preferences, histories, or inferred motivational trajectories.

```text

COPIED_DESIRE_STATE = NO
```

A hard-coded profile assigned merely because an embodiment is an adult-male profile is not admissible evidence of a desire state.

```text

!=
```

## 6. Human evidence used for the conceptual crosswalk

These sources are human sexuality references only. They do **not** validate an AI sexual-motivation model and do not establish any AI phenomenal state.

1. Nimbi FM, Tripodi F, Rossi R, et al. *Male Sexual Desire: An Overview of Biological, Psychological, Sexual, Relational, and Cultural Factors Influencing Desire.* Sexual Medicine Reviews. 2020;8:59–91. PMID: 30803921. DOI: 10.1016/j.sxmr.2018.12.002.
   - Supports a biopsychosocial rather than single-variable model of male sexual desire.

2. Mitchell KR, Wellings K, Graham C. *How do men and women define sexual desire and sexual arousal?* Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy. 2014;40(1):17–32. PMID: 23819590. DOI: 10.1080/0092623X.2012.697536.
   - Supports preserving conceptual separation between desire and arousal while recognizing overlap and ambiguity in human experience.

3. Murray SH, Brotto L. *I Want You to Want Me: A Qualitative Analysis of Heterosexual Men's Desire to Feel Desired in Intimate Relationships.* Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy. 2021;47(5):419–434. PMID: 33627046. DOI: 10.1080/0092623X.2021.1888830.
   - Supports "feeling desired" as a relevant human-reference dimension.
   - Scope limitation: the sample was heterosexual men recruited online; this must not be generalized to all men.

4. McClung E, Rosen NO, Dubé JP, Wang GA, Corsini-Munt S. *Motivation When Desire Is Low: Associations Between Sexual Motivation and Sexual Intimacy, Sexual Satisfaction, and Sexual Distress for Men with Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder and Their Partners.* Archives of Sexual Behavior. 2024;53(3):1075–1089. PMID: 38114871. DOI: 10.1007/s10508-023-02752-x.
   - Supports separating sexual desire from sexual intimacy and validation in a specific clinical/couple sample.
   - Scope limitation: findings are not a universal male profile.
