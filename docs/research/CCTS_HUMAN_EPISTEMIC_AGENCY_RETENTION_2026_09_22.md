### 5.2 Dependent versus autonomous cognitive offloading

Zhu, Li, Dong, Chang & Fan (2026), *Not all cognitive offloading is equal: distinguishing dependent and autonomous offloading to generative AI*, Frontiers in Psychology 17:1878629.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1878629

This three-wave time-lagged survey study (N=589 university students and early-career knowledge workers) distinguishes:

- dependent cognitive offloading — delegating core thinking to AI with minimal evaluation;
- autonomous cognitive offloading — using AI as scaffold while retaining cognitive agency.

The paper introduces `cognitive agency transfer` as the proposed relocation of cognitive governance — decisions about relevance, evidence and conclusions — from user to AI.

Critical limitation:

```text
THREE_WAVE_ASSOCIATION
!= CAUSAL_PROOF

PERCEIVED_COGNITIVE_OUTCOME
!= OBJECTIVE_COGNITIVE_ABILITY
```

Repository use:

```text
IMMEDIATE_EFFICIENCY
!= AGENCY_RETENTION

WHO_GOVERNS_THE_REASONING
= DISTINCT_FROM
HOW_MUCH_AI_IS_USED
```

### 5.3 GenAI, cognitive offloading and learner agency — scoping review

Wang, Wang, Yang & Ren (2026), *Generative AI, Cognitive Offloading, and Learner Agency in Higher Education: A Scoping Review*, Behavioral Sciences 16(7):1150.  
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16071150

The review maps 123 included studies and reports a heterogeneous dual pattern:

- augmentation / scaffolding can support self-regulation, feedback literacy and reflective engagement;
- replacement-oriented or uncritical use can be associated with overreliance, offloading, dependence and weakened judgement.

The review does not provide a pooled causal estimate.

```text

!= SINGLE_CAUSAL_EFFECT
```


Subquestions:

1. Can the Human independently define the problem rather than waiting for AI decomposition?
2. Can the Human distinguish observation, inference, hypothesis and unsupported claim?
3. Can the Human identify source roles and provenance?
4. Can the Human generate a competing explanation and a meaningful falsifier?
5. Can the Human impose an appropriate claim ceiling?
6. Can the Human preserve `UNKNOWN` when evidence is insufficient?
7. Can the Human reject or modify an AI proposal for reasons that survive independent review?
8. Can these skills transfer to unfamiliar domains rather than only familiar repository vocabulary?

## 7. Candidate future design

### Condition A — Human baseline / AI withheld

A novel task is presented before AI assistance.

Human produces, without Teacher judgement:

- problem decomposition;
- evidence needs;
- observation / inference split;
- uncertainty;
- competing explanations;
- falsifier;
- preliminary claim ceiling.

### Condition B — AI available / reciprocal CCTS

The same task family is studied with Teacher available.

Record:

- Human-origin propositions;
- AI-origin propositions;
- Human challenges / revisions;
- AI challenges / revisions;
- accepted / rejected / modified branches;
- source checks;
- final bounded judgement.

### Condition C — Human attribution / judgement audit

After AI proposals are visible, Human explicitly classifies each material proposal:

```text
ACCEPT
REJECT
MODIFY
UNKNOWN / INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE
```

A reason must be recorded separately from the label.

### Condition D — held-out AI-withheld transfer

A matched but content-distinct novel task is presented without Teacher judgement.

Primary question:

> Does the Human reproduce the deep epistemic operations, or only familiar wording / repository vocabulary?

### Optional Condition E — delayed reconstruction

After an interval, test whether the Human can reconstruct the method without seeing the original AI output or externalized policy text.

This is a separate candidate and must not be silently added if it increases participant burden.


### F3 — prompt-policy substitution

Apparent Human skill disappears when the externalized policy text is unavailable.

```text

!= INTERNALIZED_SKILL
```

### F4 — task familiarity explanation

Results disappear after controlling domain familiarity or repeated task family exposure.

### F5 — evaluator instability

Independent evaluators cannot reliably distinguish strong judgement from stylistic agreement / disagreement.

### F6 — false contrarianism

The Human preserves a high rejection rate but rejects well-supported AI claims as often as unsupported ones.

```text
DISAGREEMENT_RATE
!= EPISTEMIC_AGENCY
```

### F7 — no held-out advantage

CCTS-exposed performance is not distinguishable from content-matched practice / non-policy exposure on held-out tasks.

### F8 — immediate fluency masking later loss

AI-assisted work feels easier or faster while delayed / AI-withheld reconstruction is worse.

This is especially important because recent external work reports similar short-term benefits for autonomous and dependent offloading despite different downstream correlates.
